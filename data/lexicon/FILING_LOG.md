## 2026-08-31 — กรรม (42), ของ (41+1), ข้าว (41)

### กรรม — 42 filed, {1: 36, 2: 1, 3: 3, 4: 1, 6: 1}

Rule: กรรม is Sanskrit *karman*, "the doing", and Thai runs it in two registers
that must not be mixed. As the second element of a learned compound it is a
noun-former — "the work, craft or practice of X" — and that is **sense 1**
(การ, การกระทำ, การงาน, กิจ), which takes the great majority: every trade,
science, art and crime named -กรรม. The moral readings take only the words that
actually speak of merit and fault. **Sense 2**, the core, is action of either
colour. **Sense 3** is the deed that bears ill fruit, so it takes what can be
accrued and spent. **Sense 4** (บาป, เคราะห์) takes กรรม standing in for the
misfortune itself. **Sense 6** is the grammarians' object and takes grammatical
vocabulary only.

The check that settled sense 1: **fifteen of the thirty-six carry the phrase
เป็นศัพท์บัญญัติสำนักงานราชบัณฑิตยสภา** in their etymology — they are the Royal
Institute's own coinages for *surgery, agriculture, transaction, discourse,
drama, homicide, heredity, ownership*. -กรรม is doing the work English does with
*-ery, -ure, -ics, -ion*. That is a suffix, not a doctrine of karma, and filing
ศัลยกรรม under the moral sense would say the opposite.

Only one word in the list names both moral colours and it says so itself:
**กรรมบถ**, glossed มี ๒ อย่าง คือ กุศลกรรมบถและอกุศลกรรมบถ. It is the sole
occupant of sense 2, which had none before.

**เดชะกรรม** went to sense 4 on its etymology, which is explicit and rare:
เลียนคําว่า เดชะบุญ ซึ่งหมายถึงบังเอิญในทางที่ดี — coined against เดชะบุญ. If
บุญ is the good luck, กรรม is the bad, which is sense 4 (บาป, เคราะห์) and not
sense 3, the deed.

**No false-splits.** All 42 etymologies read `X + กรรม` or `กรรม + X`; not one
opens ยืมมาจาก. Even the wholesale Pali compounds (นวกรรม, สังฆกรรม, มหกรรม)
carry กรรม in its own meaning, so the split is right and the tag would be wrong.

Unsure, two:
* **กรรมสิทธิ์** (ownership) → 1. Analytically it could be sense 6, the object:
  "the right in the thing acted upon". I filed it under 1 because sense 6's
  own via_note reads *"Grammatical: the one acted upon, the object"* — it is
  syntax vocabulary, and กรรมสิทธิ์ is property law. If sense 6 is ever widened
  past grammar, this word moves first.
* **หมดกรรม / สิ้นกรรม** → 3. What is exhausted could be the deed (3) or the
  misfortune it caused (4). I took the deed, on the reading that the pending
  force is what runs out. Sense 4 is arguable and I would not fight it.

### ของ — 41 filed, {1: 41}; 1 tagged false-split

Rule: ของ has two senses and **only one of them can head a compound**. Sense 1 is
the noun สิ่งต่าง ๆ; sense 2 is the preposition แห่ง, and a preposition heads
nothing — the strings that look like sense 2 compounds are possessive phrases
whose second element is the owner, not new words. So the whole list is sense 1,
and the work is not choosing a sense but seeing what the sense does: ของ + an
attribute names a KIND of thing, and it is the most productive naming frame Thai
has. The kinds fall out in groups — food and its states, the physical states of
matter, things given, things whose possession is at issue, and the sacred object
— but they are one sense, not five, and inventing senses to hold them would be
inventing senses.

**เชียงของ tagged `false-split`, conf `probable`.** ของ there is the river, not
the noun: Chiang Khong stands on the Mekong, whose Lanna and Lao name is ของ, and
the dictionary's own โขง entry gives it as ร่วมเชื้อสายกับลาว ຂອງ. The RID states
no etymology for เชียงของ, so this is `probable`, not `verified` — the first
false-split in the corpus filed on cognate evidence rather than a stated
borrowing. It had already been marked `use: "toponym"` with the note *"this head
has no name-use sense to file it under"*; that note was chasing the wrong
problem. ของ does not need a name-use sense. It needs this word out of its family.

### ข้าว — 41 filed, {1: 19, 2: 22}

Rule: **ข้าว divides at the pot.** Sense 1 is the plant and its seed and takes the
grain in every state up to cooking — standing in the field, husked, milled,
pounded, popped, roasted, and the grain as goods. Sense 2 is อาหาร and begins the
moment the grain becomes a meal: every named dish, the states of cooked rice,
what is carried or poured off it, the royal register, the offering, and ข้าวของ.
Sense 3, a year of age, takes none of these — it counts harvests, and no compound
in the list counts anything.

Sense 1's RID gloss says หลายชนิดหลายสกุลในวงศ์ Gramineae — *many species and
genera* of the grass family — which is a licence, not a hedge: the other grasses
named ข้าว file there. That is where **ข้าวโภชน์** goes, the retired spelling of
ข้าวโพด, maize.

Unsure, two:
* **ข้าวเบือ, ข้าวคั่ว, ข้าวตอก** → 1, on the pot rule. They are ingredients, not
  meals, and the ingredient is the seed in a state. Someone reading อาหาร
  broadly would put all three in 2.
* **ข้าวของ** → 2. A dvandva: ข้าว the household's food, ของ everything else. If
  ข้าว there is the grain-as-wealth rather than the food, it is sense 1.

Noted, not acted on: **ข้าวปลูก's only gloss in the dictionary is อวัยวะเพศชาย.**
The literal sense — seed rice held back for planting — is the one that motivates
the euphemism and it is not in the entry. Filed under 1 either way, since both
readings run through the seed, but the entry is missing its own first sense.

### Explored — the surviving similarity filings, and what is actually wrong with them

**Correction to my own first draft of this block.** I wrote that the 637
similarity filings still standing in the corpus were "by the project's own
measurement more likely wrong than right." That was my sentence, not the
measurement's. The brief's 91%/9% figure was taken on the compounds a SOURCE had
already labelled; the survivors are a different population, and nobody had
measured them. So I measured them.

    lands on the CORE sense, multi-sense heads only
      filed by gloss similarity   105/385 = 27%
      filed by a source            41/214 = 19%
      filed some other way        233/1450 = 16%

**The pile-up is not there.** 27% against a source baseline of 19% is not the
91%-against-9% the brief measured. Whatever the surviving filings are, they are
not the runaway core-sense collapse, and I should not have said they were.

**So I read some.** Five heads whose every filed compound came from similarity,
41 filings, judged the same way I file — read the senses, write the rule, sort:

    นาม     7 / 7  right
    เกลือ   6 / 7  right   (เกลือแร่ arguable: nutrition or chemistry)
    พัน     6 / 9  right   (the 3 พันจ่า* ranks fit no sense this head has)
    ตีน     0 / 8  right
    บุรี    0 / 10 right

19 right, 18 wrong, 4 arguable — and **the rate is meaningless, because the
errors do not scatter. They clump by head, and both failures have a nameable
cause in the sense list.**

**บุรี** — sense 2 is `ใช้ประกอบชื่อสถานที่`, a **use-note**. A use-note describes
the compound's FORM, not its meaning, so no compound gloss can ever resemble it
and similarity is structurally incapable of choosing it. All ten provinces —
จันทบุรี, ลพบุรี, ชลบุรี, ราชบุรี, กาญจนบุรี and the rest — sit in sense 1, เมือง.
Sense 2 is empty on a head that exists to name places.

**ตีน** — sense 3 is two bare synonyms, `ชาย, เชิง`, and it became a magnet: 7 of
8 landed there, including ถุงตีน (a sock) and ฝีตีน (a pace), which are the
literal foot and belong in sense 1. The single word that genuinely belongs in
sense 3 is ตีนจก, whose own gloss says เชิงซิ่น — and it is the one that went
somewhere else. Exactly inverted.

Where similarity worked, the senses were lexically distinct and their glosses
were real definitions with content words to overlap: นาม splits name / grammatical
noun / the formless, เกลือ splits sea salt / chemical salt / worthless prize, and
similarity got 13 of 14. It is not uniformly broken. It breaks on a specific
shape of sense.

**The obvious generalisation does not hold, and I checked before writing it.**
Heads with a name-use sense carry 1113 place-name compounds and **98% of them are
filed correctly** — `add_nameuse_senses.py` did its job. Only 18 sit outside the
name-use sense, and ten of those are บุรี:

    บุรี 10 · ป่า 3 · ใน 1 · เมือง 1 · หัว 1 · ท่า 1 · ชัย 1

So there is no corpus-wide collapse to report. There is a short list of heads
where a sense cannot be reached by the tier that filled the head, and บุรี is the
clearest one in the corpus.

**Left for Nan, as a fork and not a recommendation.** I have not moved anything —
`--apply` only touches `compounds_unassigned`, so re-filing an already-filed
compound is outside this task's loop and outside what I was asked to do. What the
exploring bought is that the queue can now be ordered by *risk of a bad sense*
rather than by size: a head whose sense list contains a use-note, or a sense
glossed as bare synonyms, is where reading pays. บุรี, ตีน, โรง and สาย all have
that shape and all are unread. That is a filing order, not a repair, and whether
the 637 are ever revisited is yours.

**เครื่อง** — the ladder thing/set/implement/accompaniment/royal, each rung with a
test. Sense 3 takes what you OPERATE, sense 2 what forms a CLASS of goods (you do
not operate spices), sense 4 what comes WITH a dish, sense 5 the royal register,
which is marked on the face of the word by ทรง, ราช or พระ.

**คำ** — the line is sense 2 against sense 3: a word IN THE WORLD (คำถาม, คำสั่ง,
คำพิพากษา, กลับคำ) against the grammarian\'s คำ-, a CLASS OF WORD inside a
description of the language (คำเป็น, คำตาย, คำประสม, คำยืม, หน่วยคำ). In the mouth
or in the grammar book. **คำรบ tagged false-split** — เทียบเขมร គំរប់, where the
คำ- is Khmer\'s nominalising infix and the gloss ครั้งที่ has nothing to do with
speech. **คำนับ and ประคำ left unfiled**: neither fits any sense of คำ, and the
dictionary gives no etymology for either, so there is nothing to tag them with.

**มือ** — the limb against the PERSON. Sense 6 is the agent (มือปืน, มือมีด,
มือมืด, ลูกมือ); the limb keeps its own figurative reach without a sense of its
own, so เงื้อมมือ, น้ำมือ, มือสะอาด, ฝีมือ and ลงมือ all stay in sense 1. Sense 2,
the implement standing in for the hand, was empty and only เครื่องมือ earns it.

**ยา — the subtraction was the work.** Ten of thirty-five are not ยา at all, and
the dictionary says so for five of them outright:

**น้ำยา is the one to notice: the dictionary denies the split in as many words** —

source stating a negative, which is rarer and more useful than a stated
etymology, and it is worth a grep of its own: any entry whose etymology contains
มิได้ประสม is a false-split the corpus can find without reading. Five more I
tagged `probable` naming the source myself — รถยา < รถ्या rathyā, ชายา < jāyā,

no etymology and no medical content.

**เงิน** — silver first, money second, then the RID\'s own split of physical
against abstract: sense 3 is the coin and note in the hand (เงินปลีก, เงินแป,
เงินทอน, แตกเงิน, เงินสด), sense 4 is money as the medium and the economic life

of price, and no compound here names the measuring rather than the money. An

**ขวัญ** — this head was settled by its two source filings rather than by me.

### The บุรี defect, run to ground: it is ONE regex anchor, and it is small

province with a parenthetical head-marker and a district without one:

หลักฐาน, หลักสูตร, หลักทรัพย์, ประโยคหลัก and เสียหลัก. Meanwhile sense 6, the

**วัน sense 6 is a different word: Sanskrit *vana*, forest.** It was empty until

### A new false-split category: the คำสร้อย

**สีสัน** carries the etymology `สี + (สัน เป็นคำสร้อย), เดิมใช้เข้าคู่กับ วรรณะ
เป็น สีสันวรรณะ`. The สัน is a **คำสร้อย — a meaningless rhyming filler syllable**,
not a morpheme at all. This is a fifth kind of false split, distinct from the four
already recorded:

    1. a whole borrowing            เมตตา, มารยา, ขาวม้า, สันดาน
    2. a single inherited morpheme  หนอก, หงอก, โหนก        (remainder is not a word)
    3. a boundary error             หากว่า, หูกวาง, นามกร   (both pieces are words, cut wrong)
    4. a clipping or abbreviation   สิงหา, มีนา, นกม.
    5. a คำสร้อย filler             สีสัน                   (the piece means nothing)

Category 5 is the one no amount of reading the gloss would catch, because the
filler syllable is real Thai and looks like a word. Only the etymology says so.

### เก — eleven of twelve were never เก

The most lopsided head so far, and the cause is structural rather than

### A NEW false-split mechanism: the mis-cut consonant cluster

Under ลง, three of the 26 candidates were not compounds and not borrowings
either: **แพลง, โคลง, แปลง**. Each is ONE syllable whose onset is a cluster —
พล-, คล-, ปล- — and the splitter cut between the two consonants, leaving a
phantom ลง. This is a different failure from the one the brief describes. The
brief's test is the borrowing test (does the etymology open with ยืมมาจาก), and
it does not fire here: these words are native.

**แปลง is the clean proof.** The dictionary says ไม่ทราบรากศัพท์ and gives the
cognates as คำเมือง แปง and Lao ແປງ — **with no ล at all**. A real ลง morpheme
would survive into Northern Thai and Lao. It does not, because there is none.

**The signature, and it is mechanical:** a LEADING vowel (เ แ โ ใ ไ) stranded on
a two-character part 0, with part 1 opening in ร, ล or ว — the three consonants
that can be second in a Thai cluster. Run over every unfiled compound in the
corpus it returns **34 words**. Cross-checking each against the dictionary's own
etymology (does it name these two parts?) splits them cleanly:

    dictionary does NOT name the parts   32     cluster mis-splits
    dictionary DOES name the parts        2     ใจลอย, ใจร้อน — genuine

The 32 are the expected shape and the evidence is strong: แหลม, เหล้า, เหลน and
แหวน each carry a single Proto-Tai etymon (*ʰlɛːmᴬ¹, *ʰlawꟲ, *ʰlenꟲ, *ʰwɛːnᴬ¹),
so they cannot be two Thai morphemes; แกรด is clipped from English *graduate*;
โคลอน transliterates *colon*; แกรก is onomatopoeia; แหวะ, แหละ, แหล่ม, แหลก and
เหรอ are each marked แผลงมาจาก, altered from a single word.

**The test has at least one false positive and it must be said.** **ไขลาน** is a
genuine two-syllable compound — ไข + ลาน, to wind a clock spring, used
figuratively for nagging. It matches the signature by accident. So the signature
is a SCREEN, not a verdict; every hit still needs the etymology check and a read.

**Not bulk-tagged.** The 32 sit under heads (แห, เห, แก, โค, ละ, ลาน, ลม, ลอย,
ร้อน) that have not come up in the queue yet, and tagging them now would be
filing future heads out of order and on a screen rather than a reading. They are
recorded here so the next run to reach those heads has the test in hand.

sense 2 the transitive PUT DOWN ONTO, which covers two families that look
unrelated and are not (the craft coatings ลงรัก, ลงยา, ลงสี, where a substance
goes onto a surface; and the contributions ลงขัน, ลงทุน, ลงแรง, ลงแขก, where a

entering a new ERA-year rather than a new year, it moves to 2.

### คิด (khit) — 6 senses, 17 filed {1: 4, 2: 5, 4: 2, 5: 3, 6: 3}

**Rule.** The six senses divide by WHAT the thinking produces. Sense 1 is the making
— a thought composed in the mind — so the nouns of a formed idea and the verbs of
devising and originating file there. Sense 2 is the turning-over: hard or repeated
deliberation, and anything offered to be deliberated on. Sense 4 is reckoning with
numbers. Sense 5 is thinking AT something: an intention formed against a person or
against oneself. Sense 6 is bare นึก — held in mind or arriving in it, no work done.

Sense 3 คาดคะเน, to estimate, took nothing. None of the seventeen is about guessing.

**The one filing with a source-shaped reason: ลูกคิด at 4.** The abacus's gloss opens
เครื่องคำนวณเลข and คำนวณ is sense 4's entire gloss. คิดบัญชี joins it, reckoning an
account, with the revenge sense riding on the reckoning.

**Unsure — คิดค้น, filed 1.** Two glosses: ตริตรองเพื่อหาความจริง (pondering toward a
truth) and คิดประดิษฐ์บางสิ่งขึ้นมา (devising something new). The first is sense 2 word
for word and the second is sense 1. I filed 1 because what the word does in the
language is *invent*, and put ต้นคิด, the originator, beside it. Decide the other way
and both move to 2.

**Unsure — คิดสั้น, filed 5.** Its gloss is คิดทำลายตนเอง — an intention formed against
oneself, which is sense 5. But คิดสั้น stands opposite คิดยาว, and that pair is about
the LENGTH of deliberation, which is sense 2. I took the gloss over the pair.

**Unsure — ข้อคิด, filed 2.** "ประเด็นที่เสนอให้คิด" — a point put up to be thought
about, so I filed it with the deliberation. It could sit at 1 beside ความคิด as
simply a formed thought, which is how it is used when it means a moral or takeaway.

**Noted — สมคิด, filed 6.** A given name, meaning as-one-had-in-mind. It belongs to
the สมใจ / สมนึก family, and สมนึก pairs with นึก, which is sense 6's whole gloss. The
head has no naming sense and I did not invent one.

### ตาย (tai) — 8 senses, 17 filed {1: 7, 2: 5, 4: 4, 7: 1}

**Rule.** The chain runs death (1) → a body that cannot move (2) → a value that will
not change (4), and a compound files by which of the three it is about. Sense 1 keeps
everything where something really dies or is killed, figurative uses included — the
friend you would die for, the tree dead on its feet, the finishing blow. Sense 2 takes
what is inert or unfeeling. Sense 4, whose existing member ตายตัว *is* fixedness,
takes what is settled and will not shift.

Senses 3, 5 and 6 — the stopped machine, the gambling gate, the foul in a game — took
nothing.

**Unsure — ไพ่ตาย, filed 4.** A card stuck in the hand that nobody will discard to
you, and by extension the secret that can ruin someone. Sense 5 is the nearest thing
the head has to a gambling sense, but its gloss is specifically about ประตู in โป and
ถั่ว, dice games, not cards. I filed the parent sense, 4, rather than force a card
into a dice gloss. Someone who knows the game may say 5 is right.

**Unsure — คำตาย, filed 2.** The dead syllable of Thai prosody, against คำเป็น. I read
it as the sound that cannot go on, which is the not-moving sense. It is the only
technical grammar term in the set and I have no source placing it.

**Unsure — เงื่อนตาย at 2 against ปิดตาย at 4.** I split these on physical-versus-
settled: a knot that will not slip is an immobile object (2), a door ปิดตลอดไป is a
finality (4). It is a thin line and both could sit at 4 together.

**ตายใจ, filed 2.** หลงเชื่ออย่างไม่สงสัย — the ใจ stops stirring, stops suspecting. The
competing read is 4, a mind settled into certainty. I took the literal metaphor: the
thing that died is the heart, and a dead heart does not move.

### อากาศ (a-kat) — 4 senses, 15 filed {1: 5, 3: 9, 4: 1}

### ขาด — 15 filed {1: 9, 2: 3, 3: 2, 5: 1}

**Rule.** ขาด runs in two branches off the cut. Sense 1 keeps everything where
something is SEVERED, and Thai's whole vocabulary of finality sits there, because
a thing cut through cannot be rejoined: ตัดขาด, เด็ดขาด, ชี้ขาด, ผูกขาด, ขายขาด,

### ลาย — 12 filed {1: 10, 5: 1, 7: 1}, 3 tagged false-split

**Rule.** ลาย is the PATTERN and nearly everything files at 1, because Thai reads
writing as pattern: a written character is a ลาย (ลายสือ, ลายลักษณ์), so is a hand
(ลายมือ, ลายเซ็น), and so is the mark a person leaves on the world for good or ill

**Three false-splits, all from thai-full etymologies:**

* **ทำลาย** — `แผลงมาจาก ทลาย, จากเขมรเก่าสมัยอังกอร์ ទ្លាយ៑`. The whole word is an

* **น้ำลาย** — `น้ำ + ลาย; "ลาย" คำนี้มาจากไทดั้งเดิม *laːjᴬ`, where the pattern
