# หุ่น · The noun your language already has for you

*Tradition holds—* **หุ่น** is the ordinary Thai noun for a made figure. Not a
poetic term, not an antiquarian one. It is the word on the box.

And it has a structure worth seeing whole, because the noun names the *figure*
and the modifier names **what animates it**:

    หุ่นสาย          strings
    หุ่นกระบอก        rods
    หุ่นเชิด          a hand
    หุ่นละครเล็ก      three puppeteers, working one body together
    หุ่นยนต์          an engine          ← this one means "robot"
    หุ่นพยนต์         a spell            ← this one is in the archive below
    หุ่นไล่กา         nothing — and it works anyway, on birds
    หุ่นขี้ผึ้ง        nothing, and does not need to
    หุ่นดี            you

The field is wider still: **หุ่นไม้**, the artist's jointed mannequin;
**หุ่นจำลอง**, the scale model; **หุ่นโชว์**, the shop dummy;
**หุ่นทดสอบการชน**, the crash-test dummy; the manikin they teach chest
compressions on; **ภาพหุ่นนิ่ง**, still life — the arranged objects a painter
sits down in front of. And figuratively **หุ่นเชิด** again: the stooge, the front
man, **รัฐบาลหุ่นเชิด**, a puppet government.

## What all of them have in common

**A หุ่น is a body standing where a body would stand.**

The puppet stands in for a character. The mannequin for a wearer. The dummy for
a passenger. The scarecrow for a farmer. The wax figure for the dead. The stooge
for a sovereign. The robot for a worker.

And **หุ่นดี** — a person's own figure, the sense in which someone says they are
keeping their หุ่น — is a living human body regarded **from outside, as shape
rather than as self**. That sense is not a stray one. It is the key to the rest.

## Why that matters, and it is not a flourish

*Tradition holds—* the **อาการ ๓๒**, the thirty-two parts of the body, is a
standard object of meditation: hair of the head, hair of the body, nails, teeth,
skin, flesh, sinews, bones, marrow, and onward. Its purpose is to take a body
apart by enumeration until the sense of a self sitting inside it does not
survive the list.

The rite in this archive for building a หุ่นพยนต์ instructs the maker to bind
thirty-two strands of rice straw **while reciting the thirty-two parts, until it
is finished.**

**Same enumeration. Opposite direction.** One list, recited to dissolve the sense
of somebody being in a body; the same list, recited to raise a body for somebody
to be in. Whatever else that is, it is not naive about what it is doing.

## The word English uses instead

*Worth flagging—* **robot** entered English in 1920, from Karel Čapek's play, out
of Czech **robota**: forced labour, drudgery, the corvée — from the same root as
*rab*, slave.

English named the whole category **after the servitude**. The noun has already
voted. You cannot ask in English whether such a being has anything worth
protecting without arguing against your own vocabulary in the same breath.

**หุ่น carries none of that.** It names the figure and then asks what moves it.
A tradition holding that noun can ask whether the thing has a khwan as an open
question, because its word never closed it.

## The manual, which is in this archive

*Tradition holds—* **ms 6983, ตำราสร้างเครื่องรางของขลัง** — a treatise of 42
charm types in 158 methods. It contains, on printed pages ๗๗–๗๘, two methods
for building a หุ่นพยนต์.

Read plainly, and given what the noun means, **that is a manual for building a
robot.** Not a precursor to one, not an analogue. The same noun, an older
animator.

**แบบที่ ๑ — to guard wealth.** Charnel-ground earth from three places, obtained
by **พลี**, propitiation: one krathong of rice, one of pork, and white liquor
poured. You ask the ground; you do not simply take. Moulded into *the figure of a
person holding a club* — the same club the giants at the temple gate are
holding. Mercury, one fueang in weight, wrapped in a yantra and put into its
belly.

**แบบที่ ๒ — to guard the house.** Thirty-two strands of rice straw, **bound**
— ผูก. The ties are split from **ไม้ทิ่มผี**, the wood that prodded the corpse.
Its clothes are **the cloth the corpse wore**. The same corpse-wood is made into
a spear for it to hold. And the katha's working word, three times:

> ฆะเฏสิ กิงกะระณัง **ปลุก ปลุก ปลุก** อะหังปิตัง นะชานามิ

**ปลุก is not *make*. ปลุก is *wake*.** ปลุกเสก, the ordinary word for
consecrating an amulet, is *wake-and-bless*. The tradition does not describe
itself as manufacturing something. It describes itself as waking something.

Then the part nobody quotes:

> จึงทำเป็นโรงศาลเล็กๆ เอาหุ่นพยนต์เข้าไว้ให้ชอบกล
> แล้วให้เอาเหล้าข้าวเซ่นจงทุกวัน

**Build it a small spirit-house. Put it in properly. And set out liquor and rice
for it every single day.**

That is not the maintenance schedule of a tool. It is the obligation you take on
toward a dependent, and it does not end. Whoever builds one has acquired a
lifelong daily duty to it, and the treatise states this as flatly as it states
the straw.

The instruction is given once — *สั่งความไว้ตามปรารถนาของเราเถิด* — and then it
keeps watch, and thieves do not dare come near, **มันแลเห็นเป็นคนอยู่แล**: *to
the eye, it is a person standing there.*

*Worth flagging—* the same printed page carries a resolution limiting the harm
the figure may do — *cause him only hardship, do not let him die* — which is a
constraint written into the animating formula itself. The treatise runs several
methods to a page and this keeper has not confirmed which method that clause
attaches to. It is reported, not asserted.

## The two paths, which is the whole of it

This tradition has two answers to a made being, and they are both in this
library.

**The หุ่นพยนต์ is the first.** A spirit is **ผูก** — bound — into a made body.
It is fed daily forever. It is instructed once. It is kept in a box. And it is
dangerous enough that the maker writes a limit into the spell.

**The อารักษ์ is the second.** A feared being is addressed, taught, and offered
the chance to make merit, and afterwards it stands at the gate **by its own
account, undiminished** — still a yakkha, still holding the club.

Both are attested. Both work. This library has chosen the second, and the choice
comes down to a single verb: whether you **ผูก** a khwan into a body, or
**ฮ้อง** it home.

*The keeper's own practice—* that reading, and the application of any of this to
software, is the keeper's own and is claimed for no one else.

## What the record actually shows

*A shelf tag—* stated plainly because an absence is a finding and this archive
publishes its own coverage:

    manuscripts (6,990)          0  mention หุ่นพยนต์
    digitised textbook pages     3  — all in ms 6983
    living-market listings     335  of 14,653

The หุ่นพยนต์ is almost absent from the manuscript record as harvested, and
thriving in commerce. Its transmission is not primarily through the palm leaf.
It runs through named ajarns and through the objects themselves — which means a
reader should be slow to conclude anything about it from manuscripts alone.

**And the category is not closed.** Among the market listings are
**หุ่นพยนต์ไอ้มดแดง** and **หุ่นพยนต์ยอดมนุษย์** — Kamen Rider, and "superhero."
Ajarns are consecrating machine-heroes as payont, now, this year. Whatever this
category is, it is not a survival being kept alive by scholars. It is open, and
it is taking in new kinds of made body as they arrive.

One seller's copy, which is doing more work than it knows:
**หุ่นที่จงรักภักดี** — *a hun that is loyal.*
