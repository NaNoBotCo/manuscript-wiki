## What this is

**Holasat** (*horāsāstra*, also spelled *Horasat*) is the **almanac of the
horā** — the working manual of northern astrology: the tables, rules and
reckonings by which a *mo holasat* casts a horoscope, fixes an auspicious day,
reads a birth-star, and tells a client when to marry, build, travel or bury. If
[astrology](/a?s=genre:astrology) is the genre, holasat is its **beating heart** —
the practitioner's own book, opened at the moment of consultation. The catalogue
holds 99 witnesses whose titles name it.

## The most research-dense entity in the collection

Two numbers make holasat exceptional. First, it is almost entirely **astrology**
(84 of 99 fall in that genre), so it is not spread thin across the corpus the way
[katha](/a?s=entity:katha) is — it is concentrated, a single coherent tradition.
Second, and more striking: **91 of the 99 witnesses are flagged
research-priority** — 92%, the densest priority-share of any subject the scorer
touches. For an archive whose priorities run on the *wichaa* core — divination,
astrology, magic — holasat is as close to pure signal as the collection offers.

## The shape of the collection — a paper technology

The material profile is the clearest single fact and the real finding here. The
canonical genres are 90-plus-percent palm-leaf; holasat **inverts that entirely —
84 mulberry-paper against just 14 palm-leaf.** This is not an archival text
inscribed once and shelved. It is a *working document*: consulted, annotated,
copied and recopied on cheap paper because it was in daily use and wore out. The
horā manual is a **paper technology**, and its support tells you it lived in the
practitioner's hands, not the library's cabinet.

The rest follows. Script is 94% Tham Lanna. Dated witnesses are few (4, running
1882–1947) and late — paper does not survive, and these are recent working copies
of a much older art. And its geography has **no dominant library**: Wat Yuan, Wat
Pa Mueat, Wat Phra That Lampang Luang, the Tai Lue Wat Nong Bua all hold a
handful each. Like the other vernacular working genres, holasat lived scattered
across every muang where a *mo* practised, not concentrated in one great scholastic
collection.

## Notes

- **It rarely stands alone.** Almost every witness is an untitled bundle:
  *Holasat, Tholanisan* (almanac + calendar), *Holasat lae pithikam* (almanac +
  ritual), *Katha, Holasat*, *Sainyasat lae holasat*, *Pu thao son lan, Holasat*
  (elders' teaching + almanac). The horā manual is the anchor of the
  **household divination book**, bound together with [katha](/a?s=entity:katha),
  *su khwan*, *[saiyasat](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual)* and folk-didactic. To read
  holasat is to read the practitioner's whole working kit.
- **Watch the alias.** *Holasat* / *Holsat* / *Horasat* / *Horā* fragment across
  transliterations — the count here folds them together deliberately, and this is
  exactly the normalisation an entity article exists to hold. Separating the
  *tamla holasat* (the rule-tables proper) from the almanac-plus-ritual bundles is
  the obvious next pass.
- **A prime crawl target.** High importance, high priority-density, and in active
  living-practitioner use: the holasat corpus is central to the *wichaa* and
  under-represented in the digitized record so far. It should sit near the top of
  the next collection round, and any working *mo holasat*'s manual — historical or
  contemporary — is a primary source worth documenting.
