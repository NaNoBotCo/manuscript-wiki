## What this is

This is the **tamnan proper** — the foundation-legend in its purest form: the
sacred history of a relic, a stūpa or a city, the story a place tells about how it
came to be holy. At 328 witnesses it sits between the larger [Buddhist
Chronicle](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Buddhist Chronicle) and the smaller
[Secular History](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Secular History), and its content
is overwhelmingly the *braḥ dhātu* (relic) and *mīöṅ* (city) legends: *tamnan* of
the relic at Lamphun, of Chiang Saen, of Haripuñjaya, of the founding of Chiang
Mai.

## A cataloguing signature worth noticing

The most striking feature of this sub-genre is not its content but its
**uniformity as a batch**, which points to a single documentary source rather than
a natural cluster. Three signals line up too neatly to be coincidence:

- **Language:** 327 of 328 carry the identical tag *Pali-Northern Thai* — a label
  that appears almost nowhere else in the collection, where the norm is *Pali and
  Lan Na*.
- **Romanization:** the titles are transcribed in a scholarly IAST-style system
  (*tāṃnān braḥ dhātu càm dàṅ*, *tāṃnān yonaka jīeṅ sèn*) quite unlike the
  phonetic *Tanakhan / Wetsantala* transliteration used across the rest of the
  catalogue.
- **Provenance:** the holdings lead at Chiang Mai (152) and name distinctive
  repositories — Wat Pa Sak Noi, Wat Walukaram, and notably the **Siam Society**
  (18) — institutions tied to scholarly collection rather than temple survival.

Together these mark the *Tamnan* sub-genre as very likely a **discrete digitized
corpus folded into the catalogue** — a different project's cataloguing conventions
carried in with its records. That is a provenance fact, not a content fact, and it
matters: counts and comparisons across this sub-genre should be read as "one
source's holdings," not as an independent regional sample.

## The shape of the collection

Almost entirely **palm-leaf (327 of 328)** and Tham Lanna, dated across
**1760–1981**. Beyond the Chiang Mai concentration it reaches Phrae, Lampang,
Lamphun and — unusually — Bangkok (21), consistent with a scholarly collection
that gathered witnesses centrally.

## Notes

- **Verify the source in the raw metadata.** Before treating any figure here as a
  regional signal, check `raw_metadata` / `source_identifier`: the uniform language
  tag and IAST titles strongly suggest these 328 share one origin catalogue, and
  the honest reading is a sourced batch, not a survival sample.
- **The relic-tamnan overlaps its sibling.** In content this material is close to
  the [Buddhist Chronicle](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Buddhist Chronicle)'s
  relic-histories; the split between them here is as much a cataloguing artifact as
  a substantive one, and merging or re-sorting the two is a live question.
- **Documentary, not efficacious.** None are research-priority. The value is the
  dense, place-anchored corpus of foundation legends — and, as a bonus, a clean
  worked example of how provenance signatures hide inside an aggregated catalogue.
