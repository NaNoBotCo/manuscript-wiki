## What this is

Wat Sung Men, in **Phrae**, is the **keystone of the whole collection** — one
enormous surviving monastic library that, on its own, supplies a large fraction
of everything the archive has catalogued. With 1,780 witnesses it is by a wide
margin the single largest provenance in the corpus, and understanding its shape
is the precondition for reading almost every other distribution in this wiki
correctly.

## Why almost every distribution points here

The reason Wat Sung Men matters so much is that it does not hold a *representative*
slice of the tradition — it holds a **scholastic** one, and holds it in
overwhelming quantity. It accounts for **43% of all [Buddhist canonical](/a?s=genre:buddhist_canonical)
manuscripts** in the collection, **40% of the [grammar & lexicography](/a?s=genre:grammar_lexicography)**,
and **22% of the [jātaka](/a?s=genre:jataka)**. When those genre articles note that
their holdings "cluster at Wat Sung Men," this is why: a single library's survival
is doing the work, and the place-counts are a **preservation artifact, not a map
of where the texts were produced**. Any provincial or temple ranking in the
scholastic genres has to be read through this one fact first.

Its physical profile is a pristine scholastic archive: **99% palm-leaf**
(1,770 of 1,780), and an extraordinary **78% carrying a date** (1,383 dated
witnesses, spanning 1549–1971) — far better recorded than the collection at
large. It also has a distinctive linguistic tilt: alongside the expected Tham
Lanna sit **380 Tham Lao witnesses and 476 monolingual Pali** — a much heavier
Pali-and-Lao presence than the vernacular genres, the signature of a learned
library oriented to scripture and its scholastic apparatus rather than to local
vernacular use.

## The tension that matters to the wichaa

Here is the crucial caveat for this project. Wat Sung Men is the biggest library
in the collection and simultaneously **one of the least relevant to the wichaa
core**: it holds only ~4% of the [astrology](/a?s=genre:astrology) and ~5% of the
[magic & ritual](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual) corpora, and just **27 of its 1,780
witnesses are flagged research-priority**. The scholastic survival bias runs
*exactly opposite* to this archive's priorities — the great library preserved the
canon, the grammar and the birth-stories, precisely the best-documented material,
while the efficacious *saiyasat* is scattered and thin across the smaller
temples. The lesson is a coverage lesson: **volume is not centrality**. The corpus's
centre of gravity and its centre of *interest* are two different places.

## Notes

- **Read every scholastic ranking against this baseline.** Before treating a
  province or temple count in the canonical, grammar or jātaka genres as
  historically meaningful, subtract the Wat Sung Men effect. Where the even
  genres — [law](/a?s=genre:law_customary), [didactic](/a?s=genre:didactic_moral) —
  show Wat Sung Men *absent*, that evenness is the genuine regional signal.
- **A model of good cataloguing.** Its 78% dated rate and clean palm-leaf profile
  make it the best-recorded slice of the collection; it is the natural place to
  calibrate what a fully-described witness looks like.
- **Not a crawl priority, but a fixed point.** Little here is at mortal risk, so
  it does not compete for the next crawl's attention — but as the collection's
  largest and best-dated anchor it is the reference frame against which the thin,
  urgent, scattered *wichaa* material should be measured.
