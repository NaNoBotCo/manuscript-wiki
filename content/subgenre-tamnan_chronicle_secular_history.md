## What this is

This is the **worldly half of the chronicle genre** — dynastic and political
history, the record of muang and their rulers rather than relics and Buddhas. It
is the smallest of the three chronicle sub-genres at 159 witnesses (against 569
[Buddhist Chronicle](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Buddhist Chronicle) and 328
[Tamnan](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Tamnan)), but it is the piece that
functions as **secular memory**: how the northern polities remembered their own
statecraft.

## What it holds

The titles are the vocabulary of dynastic history. The **pongsawadan**
(*Pongsawadan lan na* — the annals of Lanna) and the **rājavaṃsa** lineages
(*Tamnan lacawong tao phanya mueang lan na*, the succession of the lords of Lanna)
give the ruling houses their genealogies; the **puen mueang** city-chronicles
(*Puen wongsa cao nai … mueang pua lae mueang nan*) narrate individual polities;
and the biographical *phawat cao* (*Phawat cao mantale*) records particular
rulers. Named muang recur — *Tamnan chiang mai*, *Tamnan mueang nan* — each polity
keeping its own version of the story.

## The shape of the collection

Palm-leaf-dominant (134) but with a visibly higher share of undated and paper
witnesses than the canon, and dated across an exceptional span — **1742 to 2024**,
the **most recent witness in the entire collection**, a sign that secular
chronicling continued into living memory and beyond. Its geography breaks the Wat
Sung Men pattern cleanly: **Phayao leads** through **Wat Si Khom Kham (26)**, ahead
of Phrae and Chiang Mai, and the material is spread across many temples. Secular
history was kept where the polity was, not gathered into one scholastic library —
a genuinely regional distribution.

## Notes

- **The chronicle/law seam runs through here.** A dynastic chronicle that records
  a ruling house is a step from the charter that legitimises it: the same *Tamnan
  Phanya Mangrai* that anchors the [Mangrai law code](/a?s=genre:law_customary) is
  a chronicle on this shelf. Read the two together where a title names a founder or
  a lineage.
- **Distinct from the sacred *tamnan*.** Where the [Buddhist
  Chronicle](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Buddhist Chronicle) narrates relics and
  Buddha-lineages, this sub-genre narrates kings and muang; separating the sacred
  from the political *tamnan* is exactly the split the parent genre flags, and this
  is one side of it.
- **Historically dense, not efficacious.** None are research-priority; the value is
  documentary. The *puen mueang* and *pongsawadan*, muang by muang, are primary
  sources for northern political history, and the sub-genre's late reach makes it a
  living historiographical tradition rather than a frozen one.
