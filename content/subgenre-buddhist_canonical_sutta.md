## What this is

This is the **discourse basket** — the *Suttapiṭaka*, the recorded teachings of
the Buddha, and the largest single sub-genre in the whole collection at 973
witnesses. Where the parent [canonical genre](/a?s=genre:buddhist_canonical) is
the Tipiṭaka as a whole, this is its narrative and doctrinal heart: the *nikāya*
collections copied out for study and recitation.

## What it holds

The titles name the classical structure directly. The **Majjhimanikāya** (the
Middle-Length Discourses) recurs through its divisions — *Mūlapaṇṇāsa*,
*Majjhimapaṇṇāsa* — and beside it sit the **Saṃyuttanikāya** (*Sagāthāvagga
saṃyuttanikāya*) and the protective discourses that cross into liturgy (the
*Āṭānāṭiya sutta*). A large share come *glossed*: the *nisai* / *nissaya* titles
(*Nisai attha nibata*, *Nisai katha sattati nipata*) are the sutta read through a
word-by-word [vernacular gloss](/a?s=genre:grammar_lexicography) — scripture and
its teaching-apparatus bound together, which is why the sutta corpus and the
grammar corpus overlap so heavily.

## The shape of the collection

It is canonical to the core: **98% palm-leaf** (955 of 973) and the **oldest
datable stratum in the collection** — dated witnesses run **1501–1985**, reaching
further back than any other sub-genre. It is also the most heavily concentrated:
**[Wat Sung Men](/a?s=temple:Wat Sung Men) alone holds 527 of the 973 (54%)**,
with Phrae supplying 551. Read that concentration as the keystone-library effect,
not as a map of where the discourses were used — every muang recited the suttas,
but one library preserved the bulk of the surviving copies. Script is mostly Tham
Lanna with a notable 95 Tham Lao and 237 monolingual Pali witnesses — the
discourse *is* Pali, so a quarter of the corpus carries no vernacular at all.

## Notes

- **None of it is wichaa-priority — and that is expected.** Zero of the 973 are
  flagged research-priority; the *Suttapiṭaka* is the best-documented material in the
  collection, exactly the kind of canon a great library preserves. Its value here
  is as the doctrinal baseline against which the efficacious genres are read.
- **The gloss is the interesting seam.** The *nissaya* suttas are where scripture
  meets pedagogy; separating the plain Pali discourse from its Lan Na gloss is the
  obvious next pass, and would connect this sub-genre directly to the
  [grammar & lexicography](/a?s=genre:grammar_lexicography) corpus.
- **Where the katha lives.** Several sutta titles are really
  [katha](/a?s=entity:katha) — recited protective verse (*Panca nibat katha*,
  *Nitsai katha thammapata*) — the point at which the discourse basket shades into
  performed, efficacious speech.
