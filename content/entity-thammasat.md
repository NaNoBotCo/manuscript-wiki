## What this is

The **thammasat** (ธรรมศาสตร์; Pali *dhammasattha*) is the **cosmic law-treatise** —
the frame that tells a Southeast Asian legal world *where law comes from and why it
binds*. In the tradition's own telling, the dhammasattha is not made by kings but
**discovered**: a primordial sage reads the eternal law written at the boundary-wall
of the universe and brings it back for humankind. It is the **_kadi tham_** pole of
law — the dhamma-law — that stands above and legitimates the king's positive code,
the [Mangraisat](/a?s=entity:mangraisat).

## What the catalogue holds

**Twenty witnesses** carry a thammasat / dhammasat name, classed as
**[customary law](/a?s=genre:law_customary)**. Set beside the twenty-four
[Mangraisat](/a?s=entity:mangraisat) witnesses, the catalogue holds **both poles of
the Thai legal cosmos in near-equal number** — a genuinely valuable pairing.

*Inference —* that the two clusters are of comparable size is itself a finding: this
collection preserves the *whole legal architecture*, not just its enforceable half.
The thammasat supplies the theory (law as discovered cosmic order); the Mangraisat
supplies the practice (a named king's enforceable code). Having both in one corpus
is what lets the archive show law as the tradition itself understood it — a single
structure with a heavenly frame and an earthly body.

## Beyond the catalogue — law read off the wall of the world

*Tradition —* the following is background from the wider Thai–Burmese–Mon legal
tradition, **not** established from these specific manuscripts; treat as orienting,
not authoritative.

- **The discovered, not decreed, law.** The dhammasattha's defining claim is that law
  is **eternal and found**, not legislated — the sage Manu (or a comparable
  primordial seer) perceives it at the edge of the cosmos and transmits it. This is
  what gives the earthly code its authority: a king enforces the law, he does not
  invent it.
- **A shared regional inheritance.** The dhammasattha tradition runs across the Mon,
  Burmese and Tai worlds; the Lanna thammasat is the northern Thai branch of a
  **trans-regional** legal literature, which is part of why it recurs in so many
  copies.
- **The paired frame in practice.** *kadi tham* (the cosmic-law cases the thammasat
  governs) and *kadi lok* (the worldly cases the [Mangraisat](/a?s=entity:mangraisat)
  governs) are the two halves a premodern Thai judge worked between — the reason
  these two entities belong read **together**.
