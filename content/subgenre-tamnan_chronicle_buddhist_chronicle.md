## What this is

This is the **vaṃsa half of the chronicle genre** — 569 of the roughly 1,060
witnesses in the [chronicle genre](/a?s=genre:tamnan_chronicle), against 328 plain
[Tamnan](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Tamnan) and 159
[Secular History](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Secular History). *Vaṃsa* means
descent — a lineage, a line of transmission — and that is the organising idea:
these texts answer the question *how did this get here from there*, where "this"
is a relic, a teaching, an image, or the Buddha's dispensation itself.

It is the biggest single body of narrative prose in the archive, and the most
recopied. **126 of the 569 titles name a ธาตุ** — a relic or its stupa. That is
the genre's centre of gravity, and it is a physical one.

## The Buddha's life, and then everyone's death

The chronological spine starts before Lanna exists. **Twenty witnesses** carry
some form of **ปฐมสัมโพธิ**, the *Pathamasambodhi* — the Buddha's biography from
the Bodhisatta's descent to the awakening — and the three oldest are the oldest
chronicle manuscripts in the entire archive: **ms 4421 (1574)**, ms 4387 (1592)
and ms 4635 (1592), all three copied at [Wat Lai Hin Luang](/place/wat-lai-hin-luang/)
in Lampang. Whatever else was worth writing out in the north in the 1570s, the
Buddha's life story was.

Then the shelf does something the [canon](/a?s=genre:buddhist_canonical) does not:
it tells you how everybody *died*. Twenty-five titles here name a นิพพาน, and at
[Wat Sung Men](/place/wat-sung-men/) they form an obvious numbered set —
fascicle by fascicle, saint by saint:

- ms 1076 — **มหากัสสะปนิพพาน ผูกต้น**, Mahākassapa, "first fascicle"
- ms 1077 — **มหาอานันทะนิพพานวัตถุ ผูกถ้วนสี่**, Ānanda, fourth
- ms 1073 — Bakkula, fifth
- ms 1080 — **ทัพพเถรนิพพานวัตถุ ผูกถ้วนหก**, Dabba, sixth
- ms 1072 — **พาหิยเถรนิพพานวัตถุ ผูกเจ็ด**, Bāhiya, seventh
- ms 1078 — **อโสกธัมมราชนิพพานวัตถุ ผูกถ้วนแปด**, King Asoka, eighth
- ms 1075 — **มหาโมคคะลานิพพานวัตถุ ผูกถ้วนสิบ**, Moggallāna, tenth

with Sāriputta (ms 1074, 1850) and the Buddha's father Suddhodana (ms 1079)
filed alongside, and — copied out on its own at Wat Sung Men in 1841 — **ms 1984,
นางยโสธรานิพพาน**, the parinibbāna of Yasodharā, the wife the Bodhisatta left.
(The series survived the cataloguing intact but not the *labelling*: the fifth and
Sāriputta's were tagged *Sutta* and went to the
[canon](/a?s=genre:buddhist_canonical) shelf, while their neighbours in the same
numbered run stayed here — a small, checkable example of how a genre boundary cuts
through a work.)
The set repeats elsewhere: ms 2743 at Wat Mueang Mo (1866) bundles four deaths
into one leaf-bundle, and Yasodharā's own turns up at
[Wat Phra That Lampang Luang](/place/wat-phra-that-lampang-luang/) as early as
**1728** (ms 4911).

*Inference —* a genre that files ten separate death-narratives as a numbered
series is not doing hagiography by accident. It is building a *chronicle of
endings* — the dispensation losing its people one by one, which is the necessary
setup for the other half of the shelf.

Because the other half is the future. **Fourteen witnesses** of the
**อนาคตวงส์** — *Anāgatavaṃsa*, the chronicle of the Buddha still to come — sit
here, three of them at Wat Chedi Luang in Chiang Mai (ms 4793, 1797; ms 4794,
1811; ms 4795, 1854), two at Wat Phra That Lampang Luang (ms 4824, **1697**;
ms 4833, 1770), one with a full Pali commentary attached (ms 1412,
*Pali amatarasadhārā ṭīkā anakatawong*, Wat Sung Men, 1836). Read the shelf end to
end and it is a single arc: the Buddha comes, the Buddha's people die, the
teaching runs its five thousand years — **14 titles** in the archive count that
span out loud, ห้าพันวัสสา — and then Metteyya arrives. See
[Wat Chedi Luang](/place/wat-chedi-luang-chiang-mai/).

## The relics, and where the north put itself on the map

Between those two ends lies what the north actually cared about: which fragments
of the body came here, and by what route.

- **ทันตธาตุ** — the tooth-relic, **16 witnesses** across the genre, at Wat Phra
  Sing (ms 51, 1761), Wat Na Pang in Nan (ms 654, 1793), and six or more times at
  Wat Sung Men (ms 2009, 1890; ms 2016, 1852; ms 2231, 1839; ms 2243, 1841). Also
  **วามทันตธาตุ**, the *left* tooth (ms 2433, 1837; ms 3517) — the tradition
  distinguishing which side of the jaw.
- **อุรังคธาตุ** — the breast-bone relic, four witnesses, the earliest ms 778 at
  [Wat Chiang Man](/place/wat-chiang-man/) (1799) and ms 226 at Wat Phra That
  Chang Kham in Nan (1820), filed as นิทาน, a *tale*. This is the chronicle of
  Phra That Phanom on the Mekong — the Lao-side great relic, kept in Lanna
  libraries, and the archive holds four more titles naming ธาตุพนม directly.
- **ธาตุตะโค่ง** — and here is the one worth stopping on. **Eighteen witnesses**,
  and two of them (ms 783, ms 791 at Wat Chiang Man) give the full name:
  **สิงคุตตรธาตุตะโค่ง**, the relic of Siṅguttara hill — which is the
  **Shwedagon**, in Yangon. Northern Thai monks copied the chronicle of a Burmese
  pagoda eighteen times, at Wat Sung Men, at Wat Lai Hin Luang, at Wat Phra Luang,
  the earliest dated **1740** (ms 2364). And ms 6682, at Wat Ratanaram in 1871, is
  titled **ตำนานดอยทุงก่ายเขาทะโค่ง** — the tamnan of Doi Tung *set against*
  Takong hill. *Inference —* that is a claim, not a courtesy: the north measuring
  [its own relic mountain](/place/wat-phra-that-doi-tung/) against the greatest
  reliquary in the region. Relic chronicles are how polities argued about rank.
- **ธาตุห้าหลัง**, the five reliquaries; **เกสาธาตุ**, the hair; **พระบาท**, the
  footprint — each with its own small literature, and each pinned to a place you
  can still walk to. The [atlas](/atlas) and [the wats](/wats) are the right
  companions to this shelf.

Threading them all together is **พระเจ้าเลียบโลก** — the Buddha's walking tour of
the north — with **13 of its 16 witnesses** filed here, and **มูลสาสนา**, the
*Mūlasāsanā*, the chronicle of how the ordination lineage itself arrived, in four
copies (ms 793 at Wat Chiang Man; ms 2545 at Wat Sung Men; ms 5395, Wat Ban Luk,
1908; ms 6637, 1834).

The Sinhalese ancestry is on the shelf too, not merely implied: **seven copies of
the ถูปวงส์** (*Thūpavaṃsa*, Ceylon's chronicle of the stupa) sit here — the
earliest ms 5030 at Wat Ban Ueam, Lampang, **1715** — alongside the
**มหาวงส์** (ms 639, Wat Na Pang, Nan, 1813; ms 3407, 1881). The Lanna *vaṃsa* was
built on a Sri Lankan model and the model is filed beside the copies.

Ten titles are **วัตถุ** tales — *Mae na kotami watthu*, *Mokkalan watthu*,
*Cotika thela watthu* — story-cases attached to a doctrinal point, where this
sub-genre shades toward the [jātaka](/a?s=genre:jataka); ten more are
**ฏีกา** commentary, where it shades toward the
[canon](/a?s=genre:buddhist_canonical) proper.

## The shape of the collection

Physically canonical — **92% palm-leaf** (525 of 569, plus 28 on mulberry paper
and 2 on khoi) — and well-dated: **376 witnesses carry a date**, spanning
**1574–1990**, one of the longest runs in the archive.

But the geography is markedly more spread than the scholastic genres, across **80
named temples**. Phrae leads (176), but Phayao (90), Chiang Mai (78) and Lampang
(73) all hold substantial shares, with Lamphun (32), Nan (31), Mae Hong Son (30)
and Chiang Rai (27) behind. At temple level [Wat Sung Men](/place/wat-sung-men/)
(128) is joined by [Wat Si Khom Kham](/place/wat-si-khom-kham-phayao/) in Phayao
(61), Wat Mueang Mo (26), Wat Lai Hin Luang (26) and
[Wat Chiang Man](/place/wat-chiang-man/) (21). Because each relic and each *that*
generated its own chronicle *where it stood*, the vaṃsa material is distributed
across the landscape of sacred sites rather than pooled in one library — the
opposite of the [canon](/a?s=genre:buddhist_canonical)'s Wat Sung Men gravity.

## Notes

- **Localised, and that is the value.** The tamnan of a named *that* is a primary
  source for the history of that site and the polity around it. Site by site, this
  sub-genre is historically richer than its even palm-leaf profile suggests, and
  it rewards a finer pass shrine by shrine rather than a genre-level
  [browse](/browse?genre=tamnan_chronicle).
- **The Takong cluster is an unworked research thread.** Eighteen copies of a
  Burmese relic chronicle in northern Thai libraries, from 1740 onward, one of
  them explicitly comparing Doi Tung to it. *Inference —* that is evidence about
  how Lanna located itself inside a wider Buddhist world, and it is sitting in
  plain sight in the title field.
- **Read beside its siblings.** Where a chronicle supplies a polity's charter it
  also touches [customary law](/a?s=genre:law_customary) — two manuscripts titled
  *Tamnan Phanya Mangrai* are filed on the law shelf, not this one. The
  [Secular History](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Secular History) and plain
  [Tamnan](/a?s=subgenre:tamnan_chronicle:Tamnan) halves are the obvious
  companions, and the Tamnan page explains why their counts must not simply be
  added to these.
- **Documentary, not efficacious — and entirely untranscribed.** None of the 569
  are flagged research-priority; the claim on attention is historical. But not one
  of them has a transcribed page in this archive. A single full
  *Pathamasambodhi* or *Anāgatavaṃsa* would be the largest single addition of
  narrative text the collection could receive.
