## What this is

The **Mangraisat** (มังรายศาสตร์, "the law of Mangrai") is **Lanna's own law-book** —
the body of customary and royal law ascribed to **King Mangrai**, founder of Chiang
Mai (1296) and of the Lanna kingdom. It is the northern Thai world's home-grown legal
tradition: rules for land, debt, marriage, theft, injury and inheritance, framed as
the settled custom of the *müang* rather than as imported scripture. Where the
[thammasat](/a?s=entity:thammasat) supplies the cosmic frame of law, the Mangraisat
supplies the **positive, enforceable code** of a specific kingdom.

## What the catalogue holds

**Twenty-four witnesses** carry a Mangrai-law name, all classed as
**[customary law](/a?s=genre:law_customary)**. This is one of the archive's more
solid clusters — a genuine, multi-copy legal tradition attested directly in the
manuscripts, not merely inferred.

*Inference —* twenty-four copies is a meaningful survival for a regional law-book,
and it signals that the Mangraisat was **actively recopied and used**, not a single
antiquarian relic. A cluster this size is worth a *variant study*: customary codes
drift as they are copied from temple to temple and court to court, so these
witnesses very likely disagree in their particulars — exactly the material a
content-level comparison could map.

## Beyond the catalogue — a king's law and its frame

*Tradition —* the following is background from the wider Thai–Lanna tradition, **not**
established from these specific manuscripts; treat as orienting, not authoritative.

- **A paired legal cosmos.** Thai legal thought conventionally splits law into
  *kadi lok* (worldly cases — the king's positive law) and *kadi tham* (cases under
  the cosmic dhamma-law). The Mangraisat is the **_kadi lok_ pole**: the practical,
  royal, enforceable half, whose authority the [thammasat](/a?s=entity:thammasat)
  half supplies from above.
- **Attributed, not authored.** As with most premodern law-books, the ascription to
  Mangrai is a **charter of legitimacy** rather than a claim of single authorship:
  the code accretes over generations of judgments and is *anchored* to the
  founder-king to give it weight. This is the same legitimating move the
  foundation-[chronicles](/a?s=genre:tamnan_chronicle) make.
- **Custom written down.** Its substance is recognisably customary — compensation
  tariffs for injury, rules of debt-bondage and land use, marriage and inheritance —
  the ordinary law of an agrarian *müang*, given fixed form.
