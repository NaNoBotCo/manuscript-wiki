## What this is

This is not a genre. It is the **backlog** — the residue of everything the source
catalogues could not place. Its raw labels say so plainly: **58% "Undetermined,"** 27%
"Miscellany," the rest "Other" or blank. Browse the titles and the problem is visible at
a glance — most read *no title (349)*, *no title (467)*: manuscripts known only by their
catalogue number, unread and so unclassified. Treat every count in this bucket as a
statement about *cataloguing effort*, not about a tradition.

Two things make it more than dead weight, though.

First, it is a **reclamation frontier**. The handful of witnesses here that *do* carry a
title leak their real identity through the same markers used elsewhere in this archive:
*Tholanisan* and *Song maha sangket luang, maha ubat* — *sangket* and *ubat* being omens
and portents — are [astrology](/a?s=genre:astrology) and divination material sitting in
the wrong bucket; *Ekasīti dutiyavaṇṇanā* is a Pali *vaṇṇanā* (commentary) that belongs
with the canon. The priority genres were partly *built* by fishing rows like these out of
"Undetermined." This bucket is the richest remaining pond for that work — and, because this
archive prioritizes the esoteric, the [Magic & Ritual](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual) and
horā markers hidden here are the first thing a reading pass should look for.

Second, its physical profile is a genuine finding, not an accident.

## The shape of the collection

This is the **only genre where paper rivals palm-leaf**: 53% palm-leaf against **41%
mulberry paper**, where every settled genre runs 88–97% leaf. That is not noise. The
*phap sa* folding-book is the support of working documents — almanacs, notes, loose
miscellany — and working documents are exactly what resists tidy classification. The paper
here is a fingerprint of the same "used, not archived" material that makes the astrology
corpus lean to paper too.

It is also the **most diverse** slice of the collection on every axis that isn't Tham
Lanna: the widest spread of scripts (Tham Lao, Shan, **Tham Lue**, Thai all appear), the
widest spread of languages (including Pali-and-Tai-Lue), and the flattest geography —
Phayao and Nan lead here, not Phrae. The odd scripts and the border traditions pool in
"Undetermined" precisely because they are harder for a Tham-Lanna-centric catalogue to read.

## Notes

- **Read it as a work queue, ordered by the project's priorities.** The first pass should
  hunt the divination, horā and *saiyasat* markers (*tholanisan*, *sangket*, *ubat*,
  *katha*, *phrommachat*) and reclaim those rows into the research-priority genres, as was
  already done for the astrology corpus. Everything else can wait.
- **The metadata is the thinnest in the collection.** Only 23% of these witnesses carry a
  date (the lowest of any genre), 48 have no recorded temple and 48 no province. Do not
  cite provenance or date statistics *from this bucket* as if they were reliable — they are
  a measure of what has not yet been examined.
- **The paper share is the one solid signal.** If a prioritization pass wants a cheap heuristic for
  "working document worth reading first," the mulberry-paper witnesses in this bucket are it.
- **It should shrink.** A healthy archive sees this genre *lose* rows over time as titles
  are read and material is reclaimed. Its size is a to-do list, and the goal is to empty it.
