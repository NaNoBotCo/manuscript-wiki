# สักขาลาย sak kha lai — the Lanna thigh tattoo

Working notes toward a page. **Distinct from sak yant** and must not be folded
into it: different lineage, different purpose, different social meaning, and
effectively extinguished by the early 20th century where sak yant is living.

## The anchor — and it is in our own corpus

**ms6968 อภิมหามนต์คาถา, p.20** (already fully translated). The decisive passage:

> ชายชาวล้านนานั้นนิยมที่จะสักยันต์ลงบนร่างกาย **จนดูเหมือนนุ่งกางเกงสีดำ**
> หรือเสื้อผ้าทั้งตัว
>
> *Lanna men favoured tattooing yant over their bodies until it looked as if they
> wore **black trousers**, or a full suit of clothes.*

The source itself reaches for the trouser metaphor. That is the whole page in one
line, and it is why "Lanna pants/shorts" is the right way in.

The same page gives:

- **Extent** — ผู้เฒ่าผู้แก่ say the old work ran สักขาลาย from the waistband
  (บั้นเอว) down to the **ankles**, torso front and back, arms, even the fingers;
  some as far as the neck and the crown of the head.
- **Enforcement by shame** — a man who did not tattoo legs and torso was gossiped
  about as being like a woman, lacking the endurance to bear the needle. *How could
  he then head a family?* If untattooed, "he might as well wear a ผ้าซิ่น like a
  woman."
- **ขันตั้ง khan tang** — the teacher's fee, set by the tattoo-master. This is the
  **Lanna** term; Central Thai practice says ขันครู khan khru. Worth surfacing:
  the northern word is the one the northern source uses.
- **Duration** — many days for a single pattern; months or years for a whole body.
- **Regional spread** — the same fashion widespread among Burmese and **ไทยใหญ่
  Tai Yai (Shan)**, "who tattoo yant no less than the Lanna."

## Terminology to get right

- The Lanna verb is **สับ** *sap* — to drive ink in with a sharp tool — not สัก
  *sak*. Check this against the corpus before asserting it in prose; 242 titles
  contain สับ but that string is common and most will be unrelated.
- **ลาวพุงดำ** *Lao Phung Dam*, "black-bellied Lao" — tattooed from the belly down
  to the knee.
- **ลาวพุงขาว** *Lao Phung Khao*, "white-bellied Lao" — tattooed only knee to upper
  thigh. The pair is a real emic distinction, not a slur-and-nothing-else, and the
  two terms mark *how far the work went*.
- **ขาลาย** *kha lai* — "patterned legs". Appears once in the corpus.

## What the corpus does NOT have

Searched: สักขา 3 pages (two are false hits — สักขาวาล a place name, มะเหสักขา
Pali *mahesakkha*), ขาลาย 1 page, พุงดำ **0**. So the yant treatises are almost
silent on it. That is itself the finding, and the page should say so plainly:
**this practice survives in murals and in ethnography, not in the manuals.** The
manuals teach the yant that went *onto* the leg; nobody wrote down the leg.

Wat Phumin, Nan is the mural site named in the secondary literature.

## It is being worked NOW — dated evidence

Field archive image **#199**: อ.จ พยัคฆ์ เขี้ยวดาบ, Pathum Thani, **31 December
2568**. Both thighs covered groin to knee — a black tiger on each, the whole
remaining field packed with dense **red** khom script in vertical columns, rows
of red ตะปู across both knees. The ajarn's own caption:

> สักยัดอักขระครึ่งขาบน ๒ ขา · ๒ ขานี้สัก ๖ ชั่วโมงครับ
>
> *Tattooed **packed with akkhara**, the upper half of both legs. These two legs
> took six hours.*

Three things follow, and they should shape the page:

1. **The form survives.** Not reconstructed from murals for a photo shoot — a
   working ajarn, a dated post, a stated duration.
2. **At the shorter extent.** ครึ่งขาบน, "the upper half of the leg", is the
   **ลาวพุงขาว** reach (knee to upper thigh), not the ลาวพุงดำ belly-to-ankle
   that ms6968 p.20 describes. The old full extent is not what is being worked.
3. **Under a different description.** He does not call it สักขาลาย. He calls it
   **สักยัดอักขระ** — "tattooing packed with script". The technique persists;
   the name has not travelled with it.

That third point is the honest heart of the page: *this is what survival actually
looks like* — the practice continuing while its own name falls away.

## Open questions before this becomes a page

1. ~~Is today's revival continuous with the old practice?~~ **Partly answered by
   #199** — it is worked now, at reduced extent, under a descriptive rather than
   a traditional name. Still open: is there an unbroken transmission, or was it
   re-derived? Ask an ajarn; do not infer.
2. The corpus is silent — so is the honest page shape an *article*, or a
   **short entry that says "here is the one passage we hold, and here is where the
   rest of the evidence lives"**? Leaning to the latter; it is truer.
3. Nan's own photographs may hold more. Not yet reviewed against this.
