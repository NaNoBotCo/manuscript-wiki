## What this is

A **phrommachat** (พรหมชาติ; northern *phromchat*) is the **household fortune-book**
— the illustrated almanac that reads a whole life off the year you were born in.
Its engine is the twelve-year animal cycle. Your year gives you a fate-figure, a
set of days that favour you and days that do not, a direction to face, a reading
for your dreams and your moles, and — the part everyone actually opens it for —
whether the person you want to marry is a year that goes with yours.

Where the [holasat](/a?s=entity:holasat) almanac is the specialist's working
table, the phrommachat is the *popular* face of the same science: the book a
family keeps on a shelf and takes down twice a year. And its most famous property
is that it is a **picture book**. In a phrommachat the image is not illustration;
the image is the text.

## The witness that was hiding in plain sight

This page used to open by reporting that the catalogue held **zero** witnesses. It
was wrong, and the way it was wrong is the most useful thing on the page.

**ms 5040 — ดูพรหมชาทหญิงชาย, "Reading the Phrommachat, Female and Male."**
Mulberry paper, Tham Lanna script, an extent of 32 pages, undated, research-priority flagged,
catalogued under [astrology](/browse?genre=astrology). It comes from **Wat Ban
Ueam in Lampang**.

The earlier count missed it for one reason: the search was run on the Central Thai
spelling **พรหมชาติ**, and this witness spells the word **พรหมชาท** — final ท for
ติ, a northern orthography. One consonant, and a text this archive holds became a
text this archive was reporting it did not have.

*Inference —* the trap has a second jaw, and it is worth naming because it is what
made the tight search feel safe. Loosen the string to พรหมชา and the catalogue
returns **nine** records — but eight of them are **พรหมชาลสูตร**, the *Brahmajāla
Sutta*, a canonical text with no relation to fortune-telling at all, sitting in
palm-leaf bundles at Wat Na Pang, [Wat Sung Men](/place/wat-sung-men/), Wat Mueang
Mo and Wat Duang Di. A loose search buries the real witness in noise; a tight
search misses it entirely. That is not a story about this subject. It is a story
about **every** count in this archive, and it belongs next to the section-level
invisibility lesson the [Khun Phaen](/a?s=entity:khun_phaen) and
[paṭiloma](/a?s=entity:patiloma) pages already teach: a zero here is a statement
about the spellings someone tried.

The title alone repays reading. **ดู** — *du*, "to look at, to read" — is what you
do with a phrommachat, not what you do with a sutta; it is the verb of
consultation. And **หญิงชาย**, "female and male," is the book's actual structure:
the classic phrommachat runs paired male and female fate-figures, one page for the
man born in a year, one for the woman. The cataloguer, writing a title line for an
untitled northern bundle, wrote down what the book *does*.

## The rest of the function, scattered under Lanna names

*Inference —* once you stop looking for the Central Thai brand name and start
looking for the **jobs a phrommachat does**, the northern catalogue turns out to
be full of it — at section level, in bundles, under vernacular titles. Every one
of the following is **mulberry paper in Tham Lanna script**, the same working-copy
profile as [holasat](/a?s=entity:holasat). Thai titles are given exactly as the
catalogue records them, northern spellings and all — ปีเกีด for ปีเกิด, ขึต for
ขึด — because those spellings are precisely what a Central Thai search string
walks straight past:

- **ms 469** — *ยันต์ คาถา และทำนายปีเกิด*, "yant, katha, and **prediction from the
  birth-year**," 17 pages at the Tai Lue [Wat Nong Bua](/place/wat-nong-bua-nan/)
  in Nan. This is the phrommachat's core operation, named outright, bound between
  [yant](/a?s=entity:yantra) and [katha](/a?s=entity:katha).
- **ms 5521** — *ยันต์ต่างฯ และปีเกีดวันเกีด*, various yant plus **birth-year and
  birth-day**, 38 pages at [Wat Si Khom Kham](/place/wat-si-khom-kham-phayao/),
  Phayao.
- **ms 5535** — *ชะตาปีเกีด ส่งแถน*, "**the fate of the birth-year**; sending to
  **Thaen**," 22 pages, same temple. *Inference —* this one carries the whole
  northern theology in a title: the reading is Indic-derived, and the remedy is
  addressed to **แถน**, the Tai sky-lord who long predates any of it. Diagnosis by
  zodiac, appeal to the old sky.
- **ms 5128** — *ตัตเสื้อผ้า, เลี้ยงผี, **ปีเอาผัวเอาเมย** และอื่นฯ*: the days for
  cutting clothes, the feeding of the spirits, and **the years for taking a
  husband and taking a wife**, 39 pages at Wat Wiang, Lampang. Marriage-matching
  by year, exactly the phrommachat's signature service, in a Lanna household book.
- **ms 355** — *โหราศาสตร์, เอาผัวเอาเมีย, สู่ขวัญข้าว*: the horā tables, taking a
  husband and taking a wife, and the calling of the rice-soul — nine pages at the
  Nan Provincial Museum. A marriage, a harvest and a calendar in one bundle.
- **ms 453** — *ส่งอุบาทรอม, ส่งขึตหลวง, ทิพพะจอนไปทาง, **ยามดี, ปีเกีด** ฯลฯ*,
  47 pages at Wat Hia, Nan: dispatching ill omen, dispatching the great *khuet*,
  the good hours, the birth-years.
- **ms 5522** — the shortest and the strangest, 8 pages at Wat Si Khom Kham:
  *วันกาตก, จาด้วยนก, เดือนคนทังหลายเกีด, ปีคนทังหลายถือดาบ* — "the day the crow
  alights; **speaking by birds**; the months in which people are born; the years in
  which people hold the sword."
- **ms 5072** — *อุปเทสปูชาเทวดาสี่ตนและ**ทำนายฝัน***, instruction for worshipping
  the four devas **and the interpretation of dreams**, 54 pages — and it sits at
  **Wat Ban Ueam**, the same temple as ms 5040 itself. *Inference —* dream-reading
  and the phrommachat are two chapters of one book in Central Thai printings.
  Here they are two bundles on one shelf. Wat Ban Ueam also holds the horā tables
  bound with Khruba Wong's record of his journey to Bangkok (ms 5062). That is a
  working diviner's kit, distributed across a temple's cabinet instead of bound
  between two covers.
- **ms 6429** — *สืบชาตายิงชาย*, "**life-extension for women and men**," at Wat
  Huai Khrai Luang, Chiang Rai: the same female-and-male paired structure as ms
  5040, applied to the [suep chata](/a?s=entity:suep_cata) rite.

*Inference —* so the picture is not "Lanna lacked the phrommachat." It is that
**Lanna kept the phrommachat's functions and did not keep its name**. The bundles
above are the fate-book taken apart into its operations and rebound with whatever
else the household needed that year.

## The twelve animals, where the corpus shows them

Two places in the archive let you see the cycle actually being used.

**The table.** ms 363 — mulberry paper, Tham Lanna, dated BE 2475 (**1932**), from
Wat Si Mongkhon (Kong) in Nan, catalogued under liturgy and titled for its
exorcisms — carries **horā tables at pages 25 to 27**. Twelve numbered columns; the
first row reads out the twelve Lanna year-names — เม็ด, สะง้า, ไส้, สี, เหม้า, ยี,
เป้า, ไจ้, ไค้, เส็ด, เร้า, สัน — and each row beneath **rotates the cycle by one
place**, with a *คำทาย*, a verdict, at the right-hand end. The verdicts are in Tham
script the transcriber could not read with confidence at working resolution.
*Inference —* rotating a twelve-place cycle against itself is precisely how you
cross one year with another and read the answer at the intersection. Whether that
particular table is matching spouses or matching a year to a journey cannot be
said until someone reads the *คำทาย* column. But the mechanism the phrommachat
prints as pictures is sitting there, in Lanna hands, on paper.

**The reading.** The [second volume of Thai Traditional Medicine](/m/thai-traditional-medicine-vol-2-6987/)
— a contributed printed treatise, so its full text is in the archive — steps out of
its pharmacology to derive a person's **constitutional element from the birth-year**
(p.139–140). Rat, Water. Ox, Earth. Tiger, Wood. Rabbit, Wood. Dragon, Metal.
Snake, Fire. Horse, Fire. Goat, Metal. Monkey, Iron. Rooster, Iron. Dog, Earth.
Pig, Water. Then the same by birth-month in threes and by birth-day, and then —
this is the part that matters — the **prescriptions are dosed against the answer**:
so many parts *sakhan*, so many parts *dipli*, "treats excess of the Tejo element."

*Inference —* that is the phrommachat's logic doing real work in a medical book.
The twelve-year cycle is not decoration on a fortune sheet; in this system it is
an index into the body.

## What the almanac says about a marriage

*Tradition holds —* the pairing tables are the phrommachat's most-consulted page
and the one everybody's grandmother could recite a fragment of. The animals are
sorted into sets that suit each other and sets that fight, usually reckoned by
their position around the cycle — years four apart tend to be read as allies,
years six apart as opponents — and each cell of the table carries a short verdict
in the same register as the day-tables: this pair will prosper; this pair will
quarrel; this pair will be separated; this one of you will not live out the
marriage. The reading is offered before the betrothal, when there is still time
for it to matter. Treat these specifics as orienting; the exact assignments vary
between printings, and no witness in this archive has yet been read closely enough
to confirm the northern version of them.

What the corpus *can* show is the machinery a match was fitted into once it had
been approved — and who, in practice, got the deciding vote. The
[Setting Up of Ceremonies, Ancient Edition](/m/setting-up-ceremonies-ancient-edition-6969/)
walks the whole sequence (p.120–123): the *thao kae*, the go-between sent by the
man's family to find out whether the woman's family will hear the suit at all —
and a note explaining that this office exists because "in former times, once a
young man and woman grew up, they had few opportunities to meet socially, apart
from festivals, traditional events, and various ceremonies"; then the formal
proposal, the bride-price and betrothal gold, the ring; then the wedding itself,
once held over two days.

And then, at p.123, the part this page is about: **"Determining the date, month
and auspicious time for the wedding — this matter is the duty of the astrologer or
a knowledgeable person to determine."** The one who goes to consult is "generally
from the woman's side." Friday is favoured because *suk*, Friday, sounds like
*sukh*, happiness. Marry in an even month, on the auspiciousness of *khu*, "pair,"
because married life is a paired life — but not the 8th or the 12th; move those to
the 9th, and best of all the waxing 9th, because "9th, waxing" sounds like
*stepping up*. **"Regarding the year: ordinarily there is no year in which
marriage is forbidden."**

*Inference —* that last sentence is a small, sharp piece of evidence. This
treatise's astrologer works from the **birth date, month and year of both parties**
— he asks for the same inputs the phrommachat uses — but he does not deploy the
animal-pairing verdict, and he explicitly declines to forbid a year. The
household's picture-book and the professional's calculation overlap and do not
coincide. *Tradition holds —* and where they conflict, families do what families
do: consult until they get the answer they can live with, or take the treatise's
own permission — one may instead "use reasoning based on convenience, in accordance
with the teachings of Buddhism, as one sees fit."

## The body-reading cousin

The archive's **only** witness in the
[divination and omen](/a?s=genre:divination_omen) genre is a contributed printed
book, and it is a phrommachat function with the almanac stripped away: the
[Manual for Reading Physiognomy — Women](/m/reading-women-physiognomy-ngo-heng-head-toe-6973/),
"complete edition, from head to foot," which sets out to divine "origin, present,
and future" from the body. Its contents page is a full inventory — the chest, the
backs of the hands, the fingernails, the lines of the palm, the body scent, the
manner of sitting, of lying down, of walking, of speaking, of eating, the navel,
the knees, the ankle-bone, the shape of the feet — and each entry is written in
the phrommachat's exact grammar: *any woman whose…* is a sign of *…*.

A square head means quick wits and self-counsel: "if she chooses the right road,
the future will meet with nothing but happiness and prosperity, but if she chooses
the wrong road she will fall into hardship and misery. This refers to every kind
of work, and also to the choosing of a spouse" (p.10). A high crown — *chom prasat
sung*, a "high palace-peak" — means ideas, steadiness, *wasana*. A palm with soft
flesh means high birth and property; the *same* palm cold to the touch reverses it
into idleness and hardship (p.115).

*Inference —* the two-clause structure is the tell. Sign, then verdict, then a
qualifying condition that can invert the verdict entirely. That is the phrommachat
page-form exactly, and it is why any surviving northern phrommachat needs
capturing as **images before text**: a bare title line, or even a clean
transcription, loses the layout that carries the argument.

## Notes

- **A prime image-store target.** Because a phrommachat *is* its illustrations, ms
  5040 should be captured as **imagery first** — 32 pages of paired female and
  male fate-figures would be one of the most immediately legible things in the
  whole collection, and it is exactly what Phase-2 page-scanning exists for. It is
  currently title-level only: the catalogue knows it exists and has never seen
  inside it.
- **Then the cluster around it.** ms 469, 5521, 5535, 5128, 5522 and 5072 are the
  same subject under other names, all on paper, all in Tham Lanna, all still
  unread. Between them they hold the birth-year fate, the marriage year, the dream
  readings and the bird omens — the whole phrommachat, disassembled across Nan,
  Phayao and Lampang.
- **Never state a count without sweeping the spellings.** This page's old *n = 0*
  was an artefact of one vowel-ending, compounded by an alias list that carried
  only the Central Thai romanisations and never *phommacat*, the form this
  catalogue actually uses. The corrected sweep — พรหมชา / พรมชา / phrommacha /
  phromcha / phommacat, setting aside the eight *Brahmajāla Sutta* records that
  the Thai substring drags in and the romanisation cleanly excludes — returns
  exactly **one** true witness. That is the argument for curated aliases over raw
  substrings, and for publishing the query beside every number so the next reader
  can widen it.
- **Read alongside its neighbours.** [Holasat](/a?s=entity:holasat) is the
  specialist's version of the same reckoning and carries the material profile this
  page's witnesses share; the [lersi](/a?s=entity:lersi) is who both of them claim
  their science from; the [divination pages](/divination) collect the working
  tools. The wider [astrology corpus](/a?s=genre:astrology) is 388 witnesses deep
  and mostly untitled — which, on the evidence of ms 5040, is the likeliest place
  for the next phrommachat to be sitting.
