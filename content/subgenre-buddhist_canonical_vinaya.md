## What this is

This is the **discipline basket** — the *Vinayapiṭaka*, the monastic code: the
rules by which the saṅgha governs itself, and the second-largest canonical
sub-genre at 494 witnesses. But the striking thing about the Lanna vinaya is that
a large part of it is not *rules to be read* but **liturgy to be performed** — the
working ritual of monastic life.

## Two faces: the code and the rite

On the scholastic side sit the classical texts: the **Pācittiya** (the offences
requiring expiation), the **Pārivāra** (the appendix-digest), the **Mahāvagga**,
and above all the great commentary, the **Samantapāsādika** (*Samantapāsādika*,
*Samanta*) — the vinaya as a body of law and its exegesis.

On the *performed* side sit the **ordination texts**, and these are the living
end of the basket: the **kammavācā** (*Kammawa*, *Nangsue kammavācā*) — the formal
acts of the saṅgha — and the **upasampadā** ordination liturgy (*Witthi
uppasombot*, "the procedure for higher ordination"). These are vinaya *as spoken
rite*: the exact words by which a novice becomes a monk. That is why this
sub-genre reaches across into the [chanting liturgy](/a?s=genre:liturgy_chanting) —
the kammavācā is a recitation before it is a rule.

## The shape of the collection

Physically canonical — **93% palm-leaf** (461 of 494) — and well-dated, 378
witnesses spanning **1537–1973**. Like the [sutta](/a?s=subgenre:buddhist_canonical:Sutta)
it concentrates at [Wat Sung Men](/a?s=temple:Wat Sung Men) (199, ~40%) with Phrae
and Lampang behind, the keystone-library effect again. Its language tilt is
notably Pali-heavy: **199 monolingual Pali** against 272 Pali-and-Lan Na — the
formal legal and liturgical Pali needs no gloss to be recited correctly.

## Notes

- **The ordination liturgy is the part that matters most to a living tradition.**
  The kammavācā and *uppasombot* texts are the operative script of an act still
  performed today; read them beside the [liturgy](/a?s=genre:liturgy_chanting)
  corpus, where the same recitational logic governs the *paritta* and *thon*.
- **Separate the code from the rite.** The *Pācittiya*/*Samantapāsādika* legal
  core and the *kammavācā*/*uppasombot* performance texts are two different
  objects under one "Vinaya" label; pulling them apart is the obvious next pass and
  would move a good share of this sub-genre toward liturgy.
- **Not research-priority, but structurally central.** None of the 494 are flagged;
  its claim is that it holds the constitutional and ceremonial machinery of the
  saṅgha — the rules that made the copyists themselves.
