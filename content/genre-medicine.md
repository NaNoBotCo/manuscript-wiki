## What this is

**Medicine** is the strangest shelf in this catalogue, because of what is on it
and what is not. Six records carry the genre — and **every one of them is a
printed book**. Not a single palm-leaf manuscript in the collection is
catalogued as medicine.

That is not because the north had no medicine. It is because of how medicine was
written down, and this page is mostly about that.

## The six on the shelf

All six are contributed printed volumes, and all six have been read into the
catalogue page by page, so unlike most of the archive their contents can be
quoted rather than guessed at:

- **[Medicine Manual of Luang Pu Suk](/m/medicine-manual-luang-pu-suk-wat-pak-khlong-makham-thao-6965/)**
  (ตำรายา หลวงปู่ศุข) — 170 pages of numbered formulas from Wat Pak Khlong
  Makham Thao, Chainat.
- **[Khao Or Herbal Recipes](/m/herbal-recipes-khao-or-lineage-6967/)**
  (ตำรับยาสมุนไพรเขาอ้อ) — its own title page claims "more than 200 herbal
  formulas, of the Southern lineage of Taksila." *Tradition holds —* Khao Or in
  Phatthalung is one of the most famous magical lineages in the south, which is
  the first hint of where this page is going.
- **[Thai Traditional Medicine, volumes 1](/m/thai-traditional-medicine-vol-1-6986/)
  and [2](/m/thai-traditional-medicine-vol-2-6987/)** (ตำราแพทย์แผนไทย).
- **[Ancient Traditional Medicine](/m/ancient-medicine-manual-6990/)**
  (ตำราแพทย์แผนโบราณ) — 182 pages.
- **[Ancient Thai Massage Manual](/m/ancient-thai-massage-doctor-manual-6991/)**
  (ตำราหมอนวดไทยโบราณ) — 194 pages.

## What is actually in them

The massage manual opens not with technique but with **anatomy of a kind no
dissection would produce**. It names ten principal *sen* — channels — and states
that each governs twelve thousand subsidiary channels distributed through the
body "for the maintenance of life" (p.13). Then it traces them. *Ittha* begins
in the middle of the abdomen near the navel, runs down to the pubic bone, along
the left thigh, back up along the left side of the spine, over the crown of the
head, and ends at the left nostril. *Pingkala* does the same on the right. A
practitioner who has memorised these routes is working a map, and the map is the
theory.

Luang Pu Suk's manual is the opposite register — flat, numbered, practical, and
completely unsqueamish. Formula after formula, each closing with a verdict on
its own efficacy: for burns and scalds, bathe the place in lime juice, "or
vinegar will also do," to draw out the poison (p.112). For a fever that overrides
menstruation, burn apple-snail shells to ash, grind fine, and mix with the clear
lime-water that is eaten with betel (p.95). For haemorrhoids, grind the
ingredient fine, mix with liquor, wrap in thin white cloth and insert — or
squeeze out the liquid and take it by mouth (p.83). A course of one preparation
runs fifteen days, after which "every kind of ache and soreness will vanish at
once."

## The seam, in one sentence

That same fifteen-day formula carries an instruction that explains the whole
genre problem:

*"While mixing this medicine, one should recite the incantation of the Five
Buddhas (that is, 'Namo Buddhaya')"* (p.19).

The pharmacy and the [katha](/a?s=entity:katha) are in the same sentence, given
by the same voice, with no seam between them. There is no moment in these books
where practical medicine stops and magic begins, because the tradition does not
hold them apart: the herbs and the recitation are two parts of one procedure,
and the compounder is expected to do both. The same logic runs through
[suep chata](/a?s=entity:suep_cata), where an astrologer's diagnosis of a
threatened life is answered with a rite rather than a remedy, and through the
[lersi](/a?s=entity:lersi) tradition, where the teacher of medicine and the
teacher of spell-craft are the same figure.

*Inference —* this is why a catalogue that sorted "real medicine" from
"superstition" would be describing a distinction its own sources do not make. The
archive keeps them on one shelf because the manuscripts do.

## Why the palm leaf is silent

So where is the Lanna medicine? The honest answer is that it is in the
collection, but not in this genre — and it takes one record to show it.

Manuscript **4516**, from [Wat Chedi Luang](/place/wat-chedi-luang-chiang-mai/)
in Chiang Mai, is twenty-two pages of mulberry paper with no title of its own.
The cataloguer described its contents instead: *ไม่ปรากฏ (โหราศาสตร์, ตำรายา,
ไสยศาสตร์)* — "untitled (astrology, medicine recipes, magic)." Three subjects,
one bundle, one physical object. And because a record gets **one**
genre_normalized value, it is filed under
[astrology](/a?s=genre:astrology) — where no search for medicine will ever find
it.

*Inference —* that is almost certainly the normal case rather than the exception.
Lanna medical knowledge travels inside mixed working compendia alongside
day-tables and [yantra](/a?s=entity:yantra), because the practitioner who needed
one needed all three. It is the same section-level invisibility documented on the
[paṭiloma](/a?s=entity:patiloma) and [Khun Phaen](/a?s=entity:khun_phaen) pages:
the text is on the shelf, but the shelf is indexed by title, and the title names
only the first thing in the bundle.

A methodological note, since it matters for anyone trying to repeat the search:
a Thai title search for **ยา** cannot settle this question, because ยา is a
substring of a great many ordinary words — จริยา, ปัญญา, พระยา, มธุปายาส all
match, and none of them is medicine. The search returns noise, not an answer.
Only reading the bundles will do it.

## Notes

- **The one lead worth pulling.** Manuscript 4516 is a twenty-two-page mixed
  bundle at a major Chiang Mai temple with medicine explicitly named in its
  catalogue description and no transcription yet. Reading it would convert this
  page's central inference into a documented fact — and would probably identify
  the search image for finding the others.
- **Six of six are printed, and that is a finding, not a gap.** The contributed
  shelf is where medicine is *legible* in this collection; the palm-leaf side is
  where it is *hidden*. Both statements are about cataloguing, not about the
  tradition.
- **Read alongside [magic & ritual](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual) and
  [astrology](/a?s=genre:astrology)** — on the evidence of 4516, those two
  genres are partly made of medicine.
