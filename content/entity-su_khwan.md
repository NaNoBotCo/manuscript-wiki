## What this is

**Su khwan** (สู่ขวัญ — "to call the *khwan*") is the **soul-calling rite**: the
ceremony that summons a person's *khwan* — the vital essence, the life-spirit that
can wander, take fright or leak away — back home to the body and binds it there
with a cotton thread at the wrist. It is the most domestic and most universal of
the northern rituals, performed at every threshold of life: birth, marriage,
ordination, sickness, homecoming, the start of a journey. The catalogue holds 51
witnesses whose titles name it, sitting squarely in the wichaa working core —
**38 of 51 flagged research-priority (75%)**.

## The khwan is called for more than people

The single most revealing pattern here is *who* gets a khwan. The titles show the
rite reaching far past the human body:

- **Su khwan khao** — calling the soul of the **rice** — is the commonest form of
  all, its own dense cluster (*Nangsue su khwan khao*, *Hong khwan khao*, *Ya
  khwan khao*). The harvest has a spirit that must be honoured and kept.
- **Su khwan khwai** — the **buffalo's** soul (*Kam su khwan khwai*, *Nangsue hiak
  khwan khwai*) — the working animal that ploughs the field.
- **Su khwan cang** — the **elephant** (*Sut lae su khwan cang*, *Lakkhana cang,
  Hiak khwan cang*).
- **Su khwan cao fa** — the soul of the **lord** himself (*Katha tang tang, Su
  khwan cao fa*), the rite scaled up to the ruling house.

This is the clearest window in the collection onto the **animist, agricultural
substrate beneath Tai Buddhism**. The khwan-world is older than the monks: rice,
buffalo, elephant and lord all carry the same wandering spirit, and the same
thread-tying rite tends them all. To read the su khwan corpus is to read the layer
of belief the Buddhist canon was laid *on top of*, still fully alive on the page.

## The shape of the collection

Split almost evenly between **palm-leaf (26) and mulberry paper (23)** — a rite old
enough to sit in the library yet worked often enough to live on paper — and
overwhelmingly **Tham Lanna (50 of 51)**. It clusters hard in **Nan (23)**, led by
**Wat Si Mongkhon / Kong (8)**, the vernacular-temple pattern, well away from the
[canon](/a?s=genre:buddhist_canonical)'s Wat Sung Men gravity. Barely dated (13
witnesses) across **1889–1973** — late, living, in-use.

## Notes

- **It rarely stands alone — it anchors the life-crisis kit.** The bundles bind su
  khwan to [suep cata](/a?s=entity:suep_cata) life-extension, *sut thon*
  recitation and *[holasat](/a?s=entity:holasat)* (*Kam su khwan nya, Kam suep
  cata*; *Hong khwan khao, Withi suep cata khao*). Soul-calling and
  fate-lengthening are two moves of one repertoire — steady the spirit, then
  stretch the span.
- **The liturgical seam.** The rite is spoken: *kam*, *hong*, *hiak* (the calling
  formulae) put it a step from [liturgy & chanting](/a?s=genre:liturgy_chanting)
  even as its purpose is efficacious. It is a recited magic — a bridge between the
  chant-book and the spell-book.
- **A living substrate, thinly recorded — a crawl target.** High priority-density
  and still performed at every northern wedding and harvest, yet under-represented
  in the digitized record: the su khwan corpus is the animist floor of the tradition,
  and any practitioner's khwan-calling text is a primary source worth documenting.
