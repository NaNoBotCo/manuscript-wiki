## What this is

This is the **constitutional memory of Lanna**: how the muang governed itself, written
down. The catalogue files all 134 witnesses cleanly as *Law*, and the titles name a
tradition with a clear two-layer structure.

At its foundation sits the **dhammasattha** (Lanna *Thammasat*) — the Mon-Burmese
root-of-law treatise that gives the northern codes their frame: *Thammasat luang*,
*Thammasat lae calit papeni*, *Thammasat lacasat* (the *dhammasattha* paired with the
*rājasattha*, the king's law). On top of it rests the **Mangraisat** — the code
attributed to King Mangrai, founder of the Lanna kingdom, which recurs here under every
spelling: *Kotmai phanya manglai*, *Manglaisat*, *Nangsue manglai*, the *Tamnan phanya
manglai*. And running through both is a recurring ideological figure — the **two wheels**,
*āṇācakra* and *dhammacakra*, royal authority and religious law "made equal" (*anacak lae
thammacak tiam kan*). Law in Lanna was never purely secular: its very titles insist that
the king's wheel and the dhamma's wheel turn together.

A nice conceptual key sits in the recurring *Lokawinai* / *Wohan lokawinai* — "worldly
*vinaya*": law understood as the layperson's discipline, the secular counterpart to the
monastic code. And at the edges the corpus shades into recorded **custom** proper —
*Kotmai lae phapeni* (law and tradition, *prapheni*) — which is why it is *customary* law.

## The shape of the collection

Its physical profile is fully canonical: **93% palm-leaf**, an all-but-total **99% Tham
Lanna**, entirely Pali-and-Lan Na. The dated witnesses run from the late 18th to the late
20th century, clustering in the 19th. But the striking pattern is geographic — see below.

## Notes

- **Perfectly even geography — and it means something.** Phrae, Phayao, Nan and Lampang
  each hold almost exactly a sixth of this genre; no library dominates, and Wat Sung Men's
  usual gravity is absent. Law was copied to *every* muang because every muang had to be
  governed by it. Better still, the codes were **localised**: *Kotmai mueang Nan* — Nan's
  own ordinances — shows the tradition adapting to each polity. The local recensions are
  the most historically valuable objects here, and the genre's even spread is a genuine
  regional sample rather than a survival artifact.
- **Two layers worth separating.** The imported *dhammasattha* root-of-law and the local
  *Mangraisat* royal code are different kinds of text bound under one "Law" label; pulling
  them apart, and pulling out the customary *prapheni* material, is the obvious next pass.
- **Law is inseparable from religion here.** The *anacak / thammacak* pairing is not
  decoration — it is the genre's governing idea, and it is telling that one witness was
  raw-labelled "General Buddhism." Read this corpus beside the [chronicles](/a?s=genre:tamnan_chronicle),
  where the same *Tamnan Phanya Mangrai* supplies the polity's historical charter, and beside
  the [didactic](/a?s=genre:didactic_moral) literature, where conduct and custom overlap.
