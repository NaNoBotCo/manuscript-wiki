## What this is

**Horā** (Lanna *holasat / horasat*) is the reckoning of time and fortune: the
lunar-solar calendar (*patithin*), the choice of auspicious and inauspicious days
(*wan di wan sia*), the casting of a birth-chart (*duang chata*), and the long
planetary periods that a diviner reads over a life. It descends from Indian
*jyotiṣa*, carried north through Mon and Burmese intermediaries and then reworked
in Tai hands, so a Lanna horā manuscript sits at the meeting point of Sanskritic
astronomy and local almanac practice.

For this archive this is a **research-priority** genre. Horā is living knowledge —
a *mo duu* still consults these tables today — which makes the manuscripts easy to
contextualise against active practice and a natural bridge between the historical
record and the contemporary vernacular.

## The shape of the collection

Horā in this catalogue is an **upper-northern** tradition. It clusters in Phayao
and Lampang ahead of Chiang Mai, Nan and Chiang Rai — the old muang of the Ping,
Wang and upper Yom basins — with the largest single holdings at Wat Chiang Man,
Wat Phra That Lampang Luang and Wat Si Khom Kham. Almost every witness is written
in **Tham Lanna** script in the mixed Pali-and-Lan Na register typical of the
region's learned texts.

The most telling pattern is physical. Where the canonical genres are overwhelmingly
palm-leaf, horā leans the other way — toward **mulberry-paper folding books**
(*phap sa*). That is not an accident of survival: an almanac is a working reference,
opened flat and read across a spread, and the concertina *phap sa* is the tool for
the job in a way a bound stack of palm-leaves is not. When you browse this genre and
see paper outrunning leaf, you are seeing the genre's *use* fossilised in its support.

## A note on the horā / saiyasat boundary

Many titles here read *Holasat* plainly, but a good number pair it with *Sainyasat*
(saiyasat, protective magic) or *katha* in a single volume — "*Katha tang tang,
Sainyasat*", "*Holasat, Tholanisan*". In practice the diviner and the ritualist were
often the same person, and a household manual bound the calendar next to the
protective verse. The catalogue's genre line records the dominant content, but the
real object is frequently a hybrid. The companion article on
[Magic & Ritual](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual) is the other half of this world.

## Notes

- **Read alongside the raw label.** A handful of rows were reclaimed into this
  genre from *Undetermined / Miscellany* by title markers (*holasat*, *horasat*,
  *phrommachat*); their catalogue metadata is thin and worth checking against the
  image before citing.
- **Dates are sparse.** Only a minority of witnesses carry a date; the span shown
  in the facts panel is anchored by those few and should not be read as the genre's
  true range.
- The *Phrommachat* (illustrated almanac of the twelve-year animal cycle) is the
  most recognisable horā sub-type and a good candidate for its own article once the
  image download phase fills in the plates.
