## What this is

**Horā** — Lanna *holasat*, *horasat* (โหราศาสตร์) — is the reckoning of time
and fortune: the lunar-solar calendar, the intercalary month that keeps it
honest, the choice of auspicious and inauspicious days, the casting of a
birth-chart, and the long planetary periods a diviner reads over a life. It
descends from Indian *jyotiṣa*, carried north through Mon and Burmese
intermediaries and reworked in Tai hands, so a Lanna horā manuscript sits at
the meeting point of Sanskritic astronomy and local almanac practice.

At **388 witnesses** it is the largest of the archive's *wichaa*-core genres —
larger than [magic and ritual](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual), and every one of the
388 is flagged research-priority. It is also living knowledge: a *mo du* still
opens tables like these today, which makes this genre the natural bridge
between the historical record and the contemporary vernacular.

## What is actually in an astrologer's book

The temptation is to imagine star charts. Read the titles and you find
something more like a **civil-engineering handbook for luck** — a working
compendium of tables for the decisions a household cannot afford to get wrong.
The catalogue's own title lines, in the astrologer's spelling, give the
inventory:

- **Finding the moment.** *Ha wan di wan hai* — seek the good day and the bad
  (ms 445, Wat Hia). *Withi ha wan di* (ms 351, Wat Si Mongkhon, palm-leaf,
  1885). *Tamla ha loek ha nyam* and *Tamla du loek nyam tang tang* — manuals
  for finding the ฤกษ์ *loek*, the auspicious juncture, and the ยาม *yam*, the
  watch of the day (ms 5517, ms 5515 at Wat Si Khom Kham, the latter dated
  1950). Thirteen titles across the genre name a *loek* or a *munla loek*, the
  "root juncture," including *Loek 27 tua* — **the twenty-seven junctures**
  (ms 2193, Wat Sung Men, palm-leaf, 1837), the Indian *nakṣatra* count carried
  north intact. *Inference —* the recurrence of 27 rather than 28 is the
  cleanest single fingerprint of the tradition's Indic descent anywhere in the
  catalogue.
- **Days that are not neutral.** The Lanna almanac does not merely rank days;
  it names their characters, and the names are extraordinary. *Kalong wan sip
  hai* — **the way of the ruinous days** (ms 4966, Wat Phra That Lampang
  Luang). *Wan lokawinat* — **the day of world-destruction** — bound together
  with the day for cutting cloth, the day for putting on clothes, the day the
  mother comes down from the house, and the first day of the house-posts
  (ms 5469, Wat Ban Luk). *Wan duean phi kin* — **the days and months the
  spirits eat** (ms 4960). *Wan ka tok* — the day the crow falls — filed
  beside *the month in which people are born* and *the year people take up the
  sword* (ms 5522, Wat Si Khom Kham). Thirteen titles in the genre carry
  *wan*, "day," in them.
- **Reading a person.** *Cata sam can, Cata phu nying* — the three-tier
  horoscope, and the woman's horoscope (ms 462, Wat Nong Bua). *Suep cata nying
  cai*, casting for female and male (ms 6429, 1939). *Cata pi koet song thaen*
  — the birth-year chart and the sending-off to the Thaen (ms 5535).
  *Tamnai pi koet*, prognostication from the birth-year (ms 469). Eleven titles
  name a *cata*.
- **Reading everything else.** *Tamla du laksana kaeo mani, Laksana dap* — the
  manual for judging the marks of a gemstone, and the marks of a sword
  (ms 2807, Wat Mueang Mo). *Lakkhana khut nam bo, Lakkhana com puak* — the
  marks of digging a well, and the marks of a termite mound (ms 5536).
  *Lakkhana kan tai, Withi tham sop lae withi sang ban* — the marks of a death,
  the method of handling a corpse, and the method of building a house, in one
  volume (ms 4964). *Tamnai fan*, dream interpretation, bound with the worship
  of four devatā (ms 5072). And *Tamla bok fai* — the **rocket manual**
  (ms 4952, Wat Phra That Lampang Luang), because Bun Bang Fai needs a date too.
- **Acting on the reading.** Six titles name a ***song kho*** (ส่งเคราะห์),
  the sending-away of misfortune — ms 5160 dispatches it to the *nine devatā*
  and worships the Four Kings; ms 5163 gives the words for pouring
  *nam som poi*, acacia water, over a person to send off an *ubat*. Eight name
  a ***suep cata***, eight a ***khwan***.

*Inference —* set the list end to end and the genre's real subject appears. It
is not the sky. It is **the moment** — the hinge at which an action becomes
safe or unsafe — and the astrologer is the person paid to locate it. That is
also why the genre is so hard to keep separate from ritual: locating the moment
and acting in it are one job, done by one person, out of one book.

## The horā / saiyasat boundary is not a boundary

Fifty-nine of the 388 titles name a [katha](/a?s=entity:katha) and 43 name a
[nyan](/a?s=entity:yantra) — *Untitled (Katha tang tang, Sainyasat)*,
*Untitled (Holasat, Tholanisan)*, *Untitled (Nyan, katha lae holasat)*,
*Untitled (Holasat, Tamla ya, Sainyasat)* (ms 4516, Wat Chedi Luang: almanac,
pharmacopoeia and saiyasat in a single folding book). The diviner and the
ritualist were routinely the same person, and the household manual bound the
calendar next to the protective verse and the medicine.

Two witnesses make the point sharper than any statistic. **ms 355** at the Nan
Provincial Museum is catalogued as *Holasat, Ao phua ao mia, Su khwan khao* —
astrology, taking a husband and taking a wife, and the
[soul-calling of the rice](/a?s=entity:su_khwan). **ms 5562** at Wat Si Khom
Kham binds monastic Vinaya, the funeral *mātikā*, a chant, and *holasat* into
one object. And the *Uppatasanti* — the great Lanna pacification chant — turns
up in this genre twice (ms 2560, Wat Sung Men, palm-leaf, 1767; ms 3127,
Wat Muang Tuet, 1925) as readily as it does under
[liturgy](/a?s=genre:liturgy_chanting) or
[magic](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual). The genre line records which content
dominated a page; the object on the shelf is usually a hybrid.

## The printed treatises: horā doing its day job

The contributed printed volumes have full transcription and translation, and
they catch the astrologer mid-consultation.

**Setting a wedding.**
[Setting Up Ceremonies, Ancient Edition](/m/setting-up-ceremonies-ancient-edition-6969/)
(p.125) says plainly whose job it is — the astrologer's — and then explains the
reasoning, which turns out to be substantially **puns**. Monday and Friday are
the favoured days, Friday especially, because *suk* (Friday) sounds like *sukh*
(happiness). The month should be even-numbered, from the auspiciousness of
*khu*, "a pair," because married life is a paired life — except the 8th and
12th, which are swapped for the 9th, an odd month, and best in its waxing half,
because "9th, waxing" sounds like *stepping up*. As for the year: no year
forbids marriage, though families with several daughters avoid marrying two in
the same year, "probably because it would be too costly." Then the page does
something no charm-book does — it tells the reader they may ignore all of it
and reason from convenience instead, "in accordance with the teachings of
Buddhism, as one sees fit." *Inference —* the calendar's authority is
presented as customary, not cosmic, by its own handbook.

**Naming a child.**
[Conducting Religious Rituals, Complete](/m/conducting-religious-rituals-complete-6971/)
(p.141) makes the astrological alphabet explicit. Letters are sorted into
groups; a man's name should open with a letter from the **Dech** (power) group,
a woman's from the **Sri** (glory) group, and letters from the **Kalakini** —
the inauspicious group — must never appear at all. The justification offered is
disarmingly sociological rather than occult: name a man "Bad," and people will
call him Mr Bad, and being called it he will become it. The same volume has the
presiding monk inscribe the host's horoscope with a stylus during a
Buddha-image casting (p.84), and requires a foundation-stone tablet with the
auspicious horoscope already cut into it before the ceremony begins (p.92).

**Reading a face.**
[Reading Women's Physiognomy](/m/reading-women-physiognomy-ngo-heng-head-toe-6973/)
runs the same logic over the body, head to toe, and its verdicts are brutal in
both directions. Ears like a cow's ears mark a woman of bitter want and
hardship, "who cannot find happiness even for the space of a single meal"; ears
level with each other, proportionate to the face, red, with a wide and deep
canal, mark a lady of rank whose husband will be a great personage (p.20). The
craft is the *phrommachat*'s, applied to flesh instead of a birth-year.

**Consecrating anything.** [Akhom Magic of Phra Narai](/m/akhom-magic-king-narai-era-6970/)
(p.143) fixes a consecration to an astrological coincidence rather than a plain
date — *"Saturday Five,"* a Saturday falling in the fifth lunar month on the
fifth waxing day. The same treatise sets out the **Itipiso of the Eight
Directions** (p.85–86), the *itipiso* verse split into a grid and deployed to
ward off harm "coming from all eight directions"; the eight-direction
[Aṭṭhīso formula](/m/warrior-vedic-katha-chai-chatri-6972/) (p.30) does the same
job in another book. *Inference —* the eight-direction schemes are where horā
stops being a calendar and becomes a **map**: the same reckoning that assigns a
character to a day assigns one to a bearing, and the [108 Na](/na/) and the
[yant](/a?s=entity:yantra) grids inherit that geometry directly.

**Even a diet.** [The Life of Luang Por Thob](/m/life-luang-por-thob-katha-he-used-6974/)
(p.50) forbids one food to each birth-weekday — water spinach on Sunday, pork
on Thursday, moringa on Saturday — with the charm's efficacy made to depend on
keeping it. Your birth-day follows you into the kitchen.

## The shape of the collection — a paper technology

Horā in this catalogue is an **upper-northern** tradition. It clusters in
**Phayao (90)** and **Lampang (79)** ahead of Chiang Mai (51), Nan (50) and
Chiang Rai (46) — the old muang of the Ping, Wang and upper Yom basins. The
archive's own [findings](/findings) rank Phayao and Chiang Rai as the two
provinces whose surviving corpus leans hardest toward astrology relative to
everything else they kept. The largest single holdings are
[Wat Chiang Man](/place/wat-chiang-man/) (31),
[Wat Phra That Lampang Luang](/place/wat-phra-that-lampang-luang/) (30),
Wat Si Sutthawat (29) and
[Wat Si Khom Kham](/place/wat-si-khom-kham-phayao/) (22). Script is
overwhelmingly **Tham Lanna** — 377 of 388 — in the mixed Pali-and-Lan Na
register.

The most telling pattern is physical. Where the canonical genres are
overwhelmingly palm-leaf, horā inverts it: **295 mulberry-paper folding books
against 85 palm-leaf**. That is not an accident of survival. An almanac is a
working reference, opened flat and read across a spread, and the concertina
*phap sa* is the tool for that job in a way a bound stack of palm-leaves is
not. When you [browse this genre](/browse?genre=astrology) and see paper
outrunning leaf by more than three to one, you are seeing the genre's *use*
fossilised in its support. The [holasat](/a?s=entity:holasat) page follows this
finding down to the level of the individual practitioner's manual.

Dates are sparse — only 38 witnesses carry one — but the span they anchor is
the longest in the wichaa core: **1578 to 1973**. The oldest is
*Athimat winitsai* (อธิมาสวินิจสย), a **determination of the intercalary
month**, from Wat Nantharam in Chiang Mai, 1578 — and it is worth pausing on
what survived. Not a horoscope, not a charm: the calendrical arithmetic that
decides which years get a thirteenth month. Two more copies of the same
problem appear later (ms 3808, Wat Duang Di, 1835; ms 2647, Wat Phra Luang).
*Inference —* the intercalation rule is the load the whole system rests on. If
the calendar drifts, every auspicious day computed from it is wrong. The
earliest thing the north bothered to preserve in this genre is the correction
term.

## A finding: the phrommachat is here after all

The [phrommachat page](/a?s=entity:phrommachat) reports zero witnesses, on the
grounds that no title in the catalogue names one. Searched by the standard
spelling พรหมชาติ that is correct. Searched by the Lanna scribe's spelling, it
is not: **ms 5040, *Du phommacat nying cai* (ดูพรหมชาทหญิงชาย) — "reading the
phrommachat, female and male" — a mulberry-paper folding book from Wat Ban
Ueam, Lampang**, filed in this genre. It is one witness, undated, and it does
not overturn the finding that the illustrated twelve-year almanac is
drastically under-represented here. But it is not zero, and the reason it was
invisible is the reason half this genre is invisible: the catalogue is indexed
on Central Thai orthography and the north did not write that way.

## Notes

- **Read alongside the raw label.** 374 of the 388 came in labelled
  *Astrology*; 11 were reclaimed from *Undetermined* and 3 from *Miscellany* by
  title markers. Those 14 have thin metadata and are worth checking against the
  image before citing.
- **Most of it has no title.** 306 of the 388 are catalogued as *Untitled* or
  *no title*, 29 of them as bare *Untitled (Holasat)*. As with the untitled
  [nyan-and-katha bundles](/a?s=genre:magic_ritual), the content is real and
  the finding-aid simply cannot see it. Section-level reading, not title
  search, is what will open this genre.
- **Dates are anchored by a handful.** The 1578–1973 span rests on 38 dated
  witnesses out of 388 and should not be read as the genre's true range; paper
  does not survive, and the paper is where most of the working almanacs are.
- **Where to go next.** The [holasat](/a?s=entity:holasat) entity is the
  practitioner's own manual seen close up; [phrommachat](/a?s=entity:phrommachat)
  is its popular, illustrated face; [suep cata](/a?s=entity:suep_cata) is what
  the astrologer prescribes when the chart reads badly and
  [thon](/a?s=entity:sut_thon) is what is recited when it has already gone
  wrong. The [divination tool](/divination), the [moon complication](/moon) and
  the [glossary](/glossary/) are the working surfaces; [browse by
  need](/need) approaches the same shelf from the client's side of the table.
- **The illustrated plates are the missing half.** In an almanac the image
  often *is* the text — fate-figures, direction wheels, animal-cycle plates — and
  a title line captures almost none of it. Filling in the image download phase
  for this genre would do more for it than any amount of further title work.
